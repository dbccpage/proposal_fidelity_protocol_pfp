S(n) numbers a derivational stage. It does not number a simplex dimension. At stage zero, <> is one opaque whole. Supplying that inscription leaves the admitted regime empty. A supplied mark admits nothing.

At stage one, <a> is one opaque token. Supply of that mark is a no-op. Admission is a later explicit event. The stage index is not a point, and it is not a one-dimensional simplex.

At stage two, reading L retains the opaque wholes <a> and <ab>. The inscription <ab> is not a pair inside the regime. Glyph identity, component count, and order are unavailable. A later grammar may parse the same bytes as two glyphs. That parser is a new interpretation, not the source token.

Stage three asks those two opaque data to be separable under a stated item cost. That cost does not force a triangular face. A stage is not a triangle. A simplicial complex can be supplied later as a realization, and a realization has no authority to create the next stage.
