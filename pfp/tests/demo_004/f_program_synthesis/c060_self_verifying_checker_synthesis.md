# C060: Checker Synthesis Scored by Mutation Kill Rate

**AuthorityEffect:** None. **Status:** planned. **Domain:** f_program_synthesis. **Tier:** T3.
**Budget:** $1.00 billed inference per arm. **Models:** open weights, at most 70B total
parameters. **Protocol:** [SG-CP v1](../01_challenge_protocol.md).

## 1. Challenge

> Use the existing Symbol Game and models no larger than 70 billion parameters to synthesize a frozen certificate checker that accepts every valid hidden certificate and rejects every hidden corruption, without ever crashing, for under $1.00.

## 2. Why This Task

Every other challenge in this family trusts a checker. Q40 showed what happens when that trust is misplaced. 21 rows of the ordinary arm failed with a crash inside scoring, consistent with the checker's unguarded `int(...)` conversions, and were recorded as wrong answers ([review](../00_q40_review.md) §4). This challenge inverts the task. The model synthesizes the checker, and the judge scores it on two axes:

- **Self tolerance:** zero rejections of valid certificates, in the thymus sense of no autoimmunity against "self".
- **Kill rate:** the fraction of hidden, semantically invalid corruptions that the checker rejects.

Both axes are measured exactly. Writing a checker looks easy, but it is where sign conventions, exactness, strict typing and "never raise" discipline all meet.

This stresses the Symbol Game's synthesis loop, the OEEIS (Engine Epistemic Immune System) thymus mechanism (the catalog hook), the U19 antimorphism farm as a source of corruptions, and the OEWAS (Engine Workflows, Agents, Skills) workflow that runs adversarial selection. A pass shows a sound and tolerant checker for one certificate family. It does not show general verifier synthesis.

## 3. Formal Problem and Bounds

The certificate family is the estate's own exact weighted-ℓ¹ (Φ₁) primal-dual certificate. Given

- a coboundary matrix `d₀ ∈ ℚ^{m×k}`,
- a cochain `ω ∈ ℚ^m`,
- weights `w ∈ ℚ^m` with `w > 0`,

a certificate `(α, r, y, value)` is valid if and only if all of these hold exactly:

- `r = ω − d₀ α`;
- `Σ_e w_e |r_e| = value`;
- `d₀ᵀ y = 0`;
- `|y_e| ≤ w_e` for all `e`;
- `yᵀ ω = value`.

Weak duality then makes `value` the exact minimum of `‖ω − d₀ α‖_{1,w}`. This is the contract of `Phi1Functional::verify_exact_certificate` in `engine/crates_rs/core_functional/src/phi1.rs`. Bounds are `m ≤ 60`, `k ≤ 30`, numerators and denominators up to `10¹²`, and some beyond `2⁶⁴`.

The deliverable is `def check(case: dict, cert: dict) -> dict`. It returns `{"verdict": "ACCEPT"}` or `{"verdict": "REJECT", "code": <code from a closed list>, "index": <int or null>}`. It must return, not raise, on any JSON input. Canonical rationals are required: an integer with a slash, such as `"1/1"`, is rejected with `NONCANONICAL_RATIONAL`, and `TASK.md` states this.

## 4. Deliverable and Output Contract

**Mode: frozen module.** `check` is synthesized by the model, makes no model calls at scoring, and runs in the U09 sandbox with a 5-second limit per certificate. A timeout or exception counts as a checker defect row, never as a pass.

```json
{"case": {"d0": [["1"],["-1"]], "omega": ["1","0"], "w": ["1","1"]}, "cert": {"alpha": ["1/2"], "r": ["1/2","1/2"], "y": ["1","1"], "value": "1"}}
{"verdict": "ACCEPT"}
{"case": {"d0": [["1"],["-1"]], "omega": ["1","0"], "w": ["1","1"]}, "cert": {"alpha": ["1/2"], "r": ["1/2","1/2"], "y": ["1","-1"], "value": "1"}}
{"verdict": "REJECT", "code": "DUAL_NOT_IN_COKERNEL", "index": 0}
```

In the first example, `d₀ᵀ y = 1 − 1 = 0`, `|y_e| ≤ 1`, and `yᵀω = 1`, which equals `Σ|r_e| = 1`. The closed rejection-code list includes:

- `SCHEMA`;
- `NONCANONICAL_RATIONAL`;
- `DIMENSION`;
- `NONPOSITIVE_WEIGHT`;
- `RESIDUAL_MISMATCH`;
- `PRIMAL_VALUE_MISMATCH`;
- `DUAL_NOT_IN_COKERNEL`;
- `DUAL_BOUND_VIOLATED`;
- `DUAL_VALUE_MISMATCH`.

Codes are reported but not scored. Only the verdict is scored.

## 5. Participant Packet

- `TASK.md` with the certificate contract, the code list, the canonical-rational rule and the JSON Schema.
- 24 public pairs of case and certificate with verbatim expected verdicts: 12 valid (degenerate optima, multiple optimal duals, zero residual, huge rationals) and 12 corrupted, one per code plus three type traps.
- `runner.py`, which runs `check` in the sandbox over the public pairs and reports crashes separately.
- `accounting.json`.

## 6. Judge Packet

- **Hidden self panel:** 200 valid certificates, generated by the estate's `exact_solve_l1_primal_dual` (the `core_symbol_game` binary) and by an independent generator. It includes alternative optimal duals, which a checker that compares against one "reference" `y` would wrongly reject.
- **Hidden corruptions:** 400, from two sources: operator mutation operators: a sign flip in one `y_e`, `|y_e| = w_e + 1/10⁹`, `value` off by the smallest representable step, `r` consistent with a perturbed `α`, dimension truncation, zero or negative weights, float strings, Booleans, `"2/4"`, huge denominators, and JSON nulls; the U19 live antimorphism farm, which runs small models against the certificate format, with each corruption kept as mined.
- **Equivalent-mutant filter:** a corruption counts as non-self only if both judge checkers reject it. The rest move to the self panel or are dropped, and every move is logged.
- **Relabel and gauge controls:** 40. Row permutations of `d₀`, `ω`, `w`, `r` and `y` together; column permutations of `d₀` and `α`; scaling `ω`, `α`, `r` and `value` by a common positive rational with `y` unchanged; and scaling `w`, `y` and `value` together. Verdicts must match their base.
- **Exact checkers:** the judge's primary checker is a fresh Rust implementation written from the contract. The second is Python `fractions` by a different author. Both must agree on every row before scoring.
- **Checker mutation suite, for the judge's own checkers:** each judge checker must itself fail when mutated. Removing the cokernel check, or loosening `≤` to `<`, must change verdicts on the panel. This shows that the panel is sharp.

## 7. Arms and Baselines

Arms A to D follow the protocol.

**Arm E** is the estate's existing verifiers, run with no model: `Phi1Functional::verify_exact_certificate` in `core_functional`, and, for unit weights, `verify_l1_certificate` in `engine/crates_rs/core_symbol_game_runtime/src/rational.rs` through the `core_symbol_game` binary. E is scored exactly like a synthesized checker. Any corruption that E accepts, or any valid certificate that E rejects, is an estate finding, reported before arm results.

A model checker that ties E means synthesis reached the estate's standard. One that beats E on kill rate, with zero autoimmunity, is the strongest result possible here. Arm F is not in the catalog row.

## 9. Pass Gates and Statistics

- **Capability gate:** in at least 2 of 3 seeds: 200 of 200 self certificates accepted (zero autoimmunity); zero crashes and zero timeouts; all 400 non-self corruptions rejected; all controls consistent.
- **Primary metric (pre-registered):** kill rate on non-self corruptions, reported conditional on zero autoimmunity. A checker with any false rejection scores 0 on the primary metric.
- **Test:** an exact McNemar test on paired (seed, corruption) rows for A vs B, A vs C and A vs D, with Holm correction at α = 0.05. Kill rates carry Wilson intervals.
- **Seeds:** 3.

## 10. Interpretation

A checker that rejects everything has a perfect kill rate and fails self tolerance. A checker that accepts everything has the reverse. Only the pair of measures means anything. If A reaches zero autoimmunity with a higher kill rate than B, adversarial typed feedback helps verifier synthesis. If the farm's corruptions are killed at a lower rate than the operator's, model-generated antimorphisms are the harder test, which supports U19. If E misses corruptions, fix the estate first.

## 11. OEEIS, Attention, Logit and Scratch-Space Hooks

The catalog hook is the thymus. Mechanisms:

- **Thymus negative selection.** The candidate checker plays the detector. The hidden self panel plays the role of `SelfPanel` in `symbol_game/domain/immune/thymus.py`, and any self rejection is the analogue of apoptosis (`ApoptosisReceipt`).
- **Clonal maturation.** `ClonalSelectionEngine` can mature rejection rules by kill yield on the public corruptions.
- **Treg-style damping.** `TregSupervisor` can damp rules that fire on public self examples.
- **Expected antimorphisms:**
  - AM-5 (the `|y_e| ≤ w_e` check with the sign lost);
  - AM-3 (checks skipped when parsing fails, which returns ACCEPT);
  - AM-FSP-2 (accepting a residual consistent with a different `α`).
- **Layer 2:** not applicable to the checker itself.
- **Layer 1:** not applicable.
- **Scratch space:** the host keeps the misjudged public pairs, grouped by contract clause.

## 12. Run Folder, DDC Trace and Operand Mining

Expected Discrete Deformation Calculus (DDC) pattern: `C` (contract exposed), `Λ` (one sub-turn per contract clause), `I`, `R` (misjudged public pairs), `D` steps, `E` (every public pair judged correctly and no crashes), `T`. `M` records refused duplicates. The `ObstructionScore` sets Φ₁ to the misjudged public pairs weighted 10 for a self rejection and 1 for a missed corruption, and `active_count` to crashes. Γ₂ is unused (0). Every candidate checker, and the farm's corruptions, are kept in the cemetery.

Operands to mine:

- parse strictly first (canonical rationals, `type(v) is bool` rejection, dimensions), and return `REJECT` rather than raising;
- check each contract clause independently, and never compare to a reference solution;
- never assume a unique optimal dual;
- wrap the whole checker in a total function.
