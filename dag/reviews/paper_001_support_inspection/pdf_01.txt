0.   The  Landau  Warning:   Why  Proposing  Is  Not  Proving
Before  you  sit  for  the  examination  in       quiz.md,  you  must  discard  the  most  per-
vasive  and  dangerous  fallacy  in  modern  computational  science:   confusing  an
optimization  proposal  with  an  exact  certificate.
Professor  Landau  stands  before  the  blackboard,  snapping  his  chalk  against  the
slate:
       *“Look  at  how  an  amateur  approaches  an  optimization  problem!   He
       takes a function, guesses an input vector, runs twenty iterations of an
       approximation heuristic,  and when the objective value stops moving,
       he  proudly  declares:   ‘The  minimum  is  3!’
       That  is  not  mathematics;   that  is  astrology.    A  proposed  candidate
       vector  gives  you  only  an  upper  bound  on  the  minimum:   Φ ≤ 𝐷(𝑠).
       If you tell me you found a potential that costs 3, you have established
       that   the   minimum   cannot   exceed   3.     But   how   do   you   know   there
       is  not  another  potential—perhaps  fractional,   perhaps  subtle—that
       achieves  cost  2.99 or  1?
       Heuristic   failure   does   not   prove   a   minimum.     In   this   examination,
       construct  an  independent  lower-bound   witness:    an  object  from
       the  dual  space  that  puts  an  immovable  geometric  floor  underneath
       the cost function.  When your upper bound from the primal proposal
       matches  your  lower  bound  from  the  dual  certificate,  the  duality  gap
       collapses  to  zero.    The  matching  bounds  prove  optimality  by  weak
       duality.    A  complete  exact  search  can  also  prove  a  minimum  when
       a  theorem  justifies  its  search  set;   the  native  spanning-forest  solver
       uses  that  route.    Here,   dual  witnesses  make  the  lower  bound  inde-
       pendently  checkable.”*

1.   The  Discrete  Graph  Model
1.1  Vertices,  Edges,  and  Cochains
Let  𝐺  =  (𝑉 , 𝐸)be  a  finite,  connected  or  disconnected  directed  graph  without
self-loops.   -  Vertices  (0-cells):   Finite  set  𝑉   =  {𝑣1, 𝑣2, … , 𝑣𝑛}  with  |𝑉 |  =  𝑛.   -
Edges  (1-cells):   Finite  set  𝐸  =  {𝑒1, 𝑒2, … , 𝑒𝑚}  with  |𝐸|  =  𝑚.   Each  directed
edge  𝑒  =  𝑢  →  𝑣has  source  𝑢  ∈  𝑉  and  target  𝑣  ∈  𝑉  with  𝑢  ≠  𝑣.   -  0-cochains
𝐶0(𝐺; ℚ):   Rational  functions  assigning  a  height  or  level  to  each  vertex:  𝑠 ∶ 𝑉   →
ℚ,  represented  as  a  column  vector  𝑠  ∈  ℚ𝑉 .    -  1-cochains   𝐶1(𝐺; ℚ):   Rational
functions  assigning  a  value  to  each  directed  edge:   𝑐  ∶  𝐸  →  ℚ,  represented  as  a
column  vector  𝑐  ∈ ℚ𝐸.   -  Absence  of  2-cells  (𝐶2  = 0):   The  graph  contains  no
filled  polygonal  faces.   Therefore,  there  is  no  discrete  2-form  curvature  𝑑1𝑐.   All
obstruction  in  Paper  001  lives  entirely  in  1-cycles  (the  1-homology  𝐻1(𝐺; ℚ)).

1.2  The  Discrete  Boundary  and  Coboundary
The   geometry   of   𝐺   is   governed   by   the   edge-by-vertex   incidence   matrix   𝐵   ∈
{−1, 0, 1}|𝐸|×|𝑉 |.   For  each  directed  edge  𝑒 = 𝑢 → 𝑣and  each  vertex  𝑥 ∈ 𝑉:


                                           ⎧−1      if  𝑥 = 𝑢 (source),
                                 𝐵𝑒,𝑥  =   {+1      if  𝑥 = 𝑣 (target),
                                           ⎨
                                           {0            otherwise.
                                           ⎩

When   𝐵   acts   on   a   vertex   potential   vector   𝑠   ∈   ℚ𝑉 ,   it   computes   the   directed
difference  across  each  edge:


                                          (𝐵𝑠)𝑢→𝑣  = 𝑠𝑣 − 𝑠𝑢

The  transpose  𝐵𝑇   ∈ {−1, 0, 1}|𝑉 |×|𝐸|  acts  as  the  discrete  divergence  operator  on
edge  flows  𝜆 ∈ ℚ𝐸:


                                  (𝐵𝑇 𝜆)𝑥  =     ∑        𝜆𝑒 −     ∑     𝜆𝑒

which  computes  the  net  inflow  into  vertex  𝑥.

2.   The  Two  Costs:   Proposal  Cost  vs.  Quotient  Minimum
In   Paper   001,   we   are   given:    1.     Edge   Reports   𝑐   ∈   ℚ𝐸:    Inconsistent   pair-
wise  measurements  (e.g.  clock  offsets,   height  differences,   ranking   advantages).
2.      Penalties   /   Weights   𝑤   ∈   ℚ𝐸>0:     Strictly   positive   rational   penalties   for
disagreeing  with  each  edge  report.

2.1  The  Proposal  Cost  𝐷𝑐(𝑠)
Given   a   proposed   vertex   potential   𝑠   ∈   ℚ𝑉 ,   the   residual   1-cochain   𝑟   ∈   ℚ𝐸
measures the discrepancy between the report 𝑐 and the exact potential difference
𝐵𝑠:


                      𝑟  = 𝑐 − 𝐵𝑠 ∈ ℚ𝐸,               𝑟𝑢→𝑣  = 𝑐𝑢→𝑣 − (𝑠𝑣 − 𝑠𝑢)

The  proposal  cost  𝐷𝑐(𝑠)is  the  weighted  ℓ1  norm  of  the  residual:

                     𝐷𝑐(𝑠) =  ∑𝑤𝑒|𝑟𝑒| =     ∑             𝑤𝑒|𝑐𝑢→𝑣 − (𝑠𝑣 − 𝑠𝑢)|
                                 𝑒∈𝐸              𝑒=𝑢→𝑣

Key   properties   of   𝐷𝑐(𝑠):    -   𝐷𝑐(𝑠)   ≥   0for   all   𝑠   ∈   ℚ𝑉 .     -   If   𝐺   has   connected
components   𝐺1, … , 𝐺𝑘,   shifting   𝑠  by   an   additive   constant   on   any   component
leaves  𝐵𝑠,  and  hence  𝐷𝑐(𝑠),  completely  invariant.   Potentials  have  an  irrelevant
gauge  freedom  ℚ𝑘.

2.2  The  Quotient  Minimum  Φ(𝑐)

8.   Halting  Pitfalls  and  Zeno’s  Trap
8.1  The  Counterexample:   Strict  Descent  Without  Termination
Consider a single edge with report 𝑐  = 1, weight 𝑤  = 1.  Suppose an optimization
routine  generates  a  sequence  of  potential  differences  such  that  the  cost  at  step
𝑡 is:


                                               𝐷𝑡  = 2−𝑡

Notice:  1.  Strict  Monotonic  Descent:  At every single step, 𝐷𝑡+1  = 2−(𝑡+1)<
2−𝑡   =  𝐷𝑡.    2.    Infinite   Rational   Sequence:   The  sequence  strictly  decreases
for  all  𝑡 ∈ ℕ without  ever  reaching  the  infimum  0 at  any  finite  𝑡.

The  Cardinal  Halting  Law:
       Strict  rational  descent  alone  does  NOT  imply  finite  termi-
       nation.
To  make  a  stopping  argument,  state  which  quantity  is  bounded  and  which  calls
must  return:
   •   If  all  costs  lie  on  a  fixed  lattice  𝛿ℤ  with  𝛿  >  0,  are  bounded  below,  and
       each admitted step strictly decreases cost, there are finitely many admitted
       descents from a finite starting cost.   Rejected attempts still need their own
       limit.
   •   A   finite   exhaustive   search   terminates   if   it   visits   its   finite   candidate   set
       without  repetition  and  every  candidate  check  returns.   Searching  extreme
       points  without  these  conditions  is  not  a  halting  proof.
   •   A finite fuel wrapper bounds attempted transitions.  A timeout or a proved
       bound  is  separately  needed  for  each  parser,  solver,  or  external  call.   Fuel
       exhaustion  means  an  unfinished  computation,  not  convergence.

PAGE 18
In  the  supplied SymbolGameGovernor, turn  counts  calls;  the  class  has  no  fuel
limit.   Demo  1  enforces  fuel  in  its  outer for  loop  and  reports UNFINISHED  while
cost   is   still   positive.     There   is   no FUEL_EXHAUSTED   member   in   the   governor’s
