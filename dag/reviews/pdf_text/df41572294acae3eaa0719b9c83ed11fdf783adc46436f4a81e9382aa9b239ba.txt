PFP III: Admitting Failure Detectors and Testing Their Effects
Jeremy H. Carroll
2026-10-02 · Version 0.1.0 · Methods paper · Author-review revision
Code baseline: 52cb5457f6c485ba548cfd292f6012a941ed22cf
Abstract
The Proposal Fidelity Protocol (PFP), previously called the Symbol Game, treats generated text
as a proposal whose admissibility depends on a declared checker. A failure detector adds another
decision: whether a recurring pattern should change how subsequent proposals are handled. That
decision can prevent a known mistake, but it can also suppress a valid unfamiliar answer. This pa-
per specifies detector admission, separates diagnostic findings from route verification, and presents
a proposed study protocol for effectiveness and false suppression: blocking a proposal that an inde-
pendent checker judges valid. Four unadmitted phrase detectors serve as worked examples; one of
them, as first written, also matched a true statement about the attractive Hubbard model. The im-
plementation contribution is a source-audited description of a bounded host and a two-signal filter,
with explicit transaction rollback, fuel consumption, and receipt replay. At the audited revision, the
canonical Demo 3 host returns NO_NATIVE_PORT for all three witness routes. Its successful protocol
checks therefore cannot establish witness acceptance or a live treatment effect. A worked batch
shows how this refusal preserves prior semantic state while recording the failed attempt. The pro-
posed experiment compares a frozen detector policy with matched reflection feedback and requires
independent route outcomes, randomized allocation, and informative safety intervals. Appendix A,
new in version 1.2.0, establishes three small exact lemmas: a second signal computed from the first
reduces the two-signal table to a one-signal detector, a Hill-type “quorum” is a threshold on a sum
and bounds no number of agents, and screening a detector against a self panel is sound for every
panel exactly when the executed predicate implies the screened predicate. It audits the repository
against them and describes one component that realizes the admission record of Section 3. No new
provider trial or general reduction in hallucination is reported.
1. Question, contribution, and source boundary
Consider a proposer asked to repair a small symbolic object. A checker can determine whether one
proposed repair satisfies a finite contract. A detector instead recognizes a possible reason that an
attempt may fail: a supplied verification flag, incomplete coverage, a reused invalid witness, or a
mutation outside the allowed carrier. The research question is whether admitted detectors improve
the rate of independently verified solutions without unacceptably suppressing valid proposals.
The contribution has three parts. First, we give a detector admission procedure whose unit is
a reproduced defect together with a valid near miss. Second, we identify what the current host
actually enforces and provide a complete refusal example. Third, we specify an experiment that
could distinguish a detector policy from ordinary reflection. Admission procedure and experiment
design are proposals; the host branches and test assertions are inspected implementations.
The implementation audit is pinned to repository commit 52cb5457f6c485ba548cfd292f6012a941
ed22cf. Repository paths below refer to that revision. Paths beginning with code/ refer to a small
re-implementation included in this deposit (Section 4.1), which is not the canonical host. Fresh

2. Related work
Reflexion retains linguistic feedback in episodic memory to inform later agent attempts. Its feedback
can originate from external or internally simulated sources. This makes a reflection control necessary
when studying a more structured detector intervention; improvement over plain prompting would
not identify which feedback mechanism helped. Shinn et al., 2023
Self-Refine iterates generation, feedback, and revision using the same language model. It supplies a
useful comparison for a proposal-improvement loop. The present design adds separately evaluated
witness outcomes and records when a filter would reject a proposal that the route checker accepts.
Madaan et al., 2023
These papers establish prior methods, not the performance of PFP. The present study has not
measured an advantage over them. Biological words in implementation names identify software
components only. The “danger gate” is the two-signal decision table of Section 3. The “thymus”
module rejects a candidate detector whose pattern matches any entry of the self panel, a fixed list
of strings treated as valid. No claim about biological fidelity or general immunity follows from those
names.
3. Detector objects and admission
A proposed detector record should contain its identifier, version, applicable route, input schema,
executable predicate, defect code, discovery provenance, and action policy. It must bind a positive
witness that reproduces the named defect and at least one independently valid near miss. A prose
warning without these artifacts remains a suggestion.
Admission proceeds through five checks. The host first reparses the offending proposal and repro-
duces the defect. It then minimizes the example suﬀiciently to identify which condition failed. Next
it constructs valid cases close to the failure boundary, including alternative notation and relabeling
where allowed. The detector is evaluated on both classes and its intended action is frozen. Finally,
the detector and fixture digests are bound into the experiment manifest. A modified predicate or
broader route scope requires a new admission record.
This procedure is intentionally stricter than counting repeated strings in a failure log. Repetition
may reflect a duplicate task, a shared prompt, or a checker bug. One thousand stored rows are not
one thousand independent examples. The repository’s self panel at the baseline has 1,000 rows but
only 40 distinct base contents, ten in each of four categories. That is a finite regression corpus, not
a statistical estimate of false suppression in open use. See the regression suite .
The actual two-signal gate offers a small operational model. Signal 1 records a pattern match. Signal
2 records a supplied structural defect, severity, and an evidence identifier. With neither signal the
decision is TOLERATED. Signal 1 alone yields DIAGNOSTIC_ONLY. Both signals produce a refusal; a high

or critical defect alone also produces a refusal. A low or medium defect alone remains diagnostic.
The implementation computes this decision table; it does not independently prove the supplied
defect or authenticate its evidence identifier. The table is only as informative as the independence
of its two inputs: Appendix A shows that when Signal 2 is computed from Signal 1, it reduces to a
one-signal detector.
For example, a pattern matching the word CUT may occur in a harmless explanation. Without
structural damage, refusing it would confuse mention with execution. Pair that match with a host-
established protected-field mutation and the declared policy refuses. The policy benefit is plausible,
but whether these two inputs are accurate in a deployed pipeline remains a separate obligation.
The conjunction tests exercise the decision table on constructed signals. They do not measure
natural-language classification accuracy.
Detector actions should be distinguished: a finding records an observation; a hold requests further
evidence; a refusal rejects one proposed transition. None grants permission to repair the object
automatically. The repair proposal and the defect report remain separately inspectable.
3.1 F our candidate phrase detectors
The package contains four candidate detectors, written as phrase lists in code/canaries.py with a
Rust port in code/rust/src/canaries.rs. A detector reports a finding when one of its phrases occurs
in the lower-cased input. Such a finding is Signal 1 in the gate above. A phrase match cannot check
a physical or mathematical condition, and it cannot tell an assertion from a quotation, a question,
or a negation: the sentence “There is no particle-hole symmetry on triangle plaquettes” matches
the AM-FSP-2 phrase “particle-hole symmetry on triangle” . None of the four detectors has passed
the admission procedure above. Each lacks a defect reproduced by an independent checker and a
set of valid near misses evaluated by that checker. They are listed as examples of what admission
has to examine.

The use-case host supplies phases, fuel accounting, atomic transaction rollback, and hash-chained
batch receipts. Each batch that the host processes costs one unit of fuel, charged before its moves
are applied; the default initial fuel is 12. If the batch is refused, the semantic state (covered elements,
witness candidate, phase) returns to its pre-batch value, but the fuel unit is not returned. A batch
with a move after VERIFY or with a malformed element identifier is refused and charged in this way.
An empty batch is also charged; it is recorded as ACCEPTED and changes no semantic state. Once fuel
5

--- PAGE 6 ---
is zero, the next batch is not applied: it is recorded as FUEL_EXHAUSTED and the host enters TERMINAL,
after which batches are rejected with REJECT_TERMINAL_STATE. The number of processed batches is
therefore bounded by the initial fuel.
The host’s state_digest starts from a SHA-256 digest of the fixture and arm identifiers. Each
batch extends it with SHA-256 over the prior digest, the proposed moves, and the host decision.
The moves are serialized as JSON with sorted keys, no white space, and ASCII escapes; this is a
declared convention, not a full RFC 8785 implementation. The digest does not cover fuel or the rest
of the host state. The receipt verifier verify_receipt_bytes accepts only the canonical encoding
of the expected 30-cell matrix (five fixtures × six arms). For each cell it recomputes the digest
chain from the initial digest, checks the fuel sequence and the scripted decision sequence for the
arm, and refuses a receipt that reports a terminal certificate or a witness type while the route port
is missing. It therefore rejects {}, schema changes, chain discontinuities, and certificate claims. A
SHA-256 chain without a key or an independently held anchor shows internal consistency; it does
not authenticate who produced the receipt.
The host’s verify_route function returns a typed missing-port refusal for any nonempty route name.
The module does not call a native board or quotient action. While the seam is missing, returning
NO_NATIVE_PORT is the appropriate outcome: it reports that verification is unavailable, not that a
witness is wrong.

So that the refusal behavior can be run from this deposit alone, the package includes a small

• its default fuel is 11, and it enters TERMINAL as soon as a batch spends the last unit of fuel;
• its fixture list replaces cochain_nonexact_cycle with frustrated_square_diagonal, and it uses
five route names, only one of which matches the baseline;
• it refuses an empty batch ( REJECT_EMPTY_BATCH), and its VERIFY step requires only nonempty
coverage and no witness;
• it represents the Arm D detector hold as a move type, not as a host method that records

• its digests use a JSON initial record, the hexadecimal form of the move bytes, and UTF-8

• its receipt verifier recomputes the digest chain and refuses certificate labels, but it does not

• code/self_panel.py builds a synthetic 1,000-row panel with the same shape (40 base strings,

For a novice, the distinction is practical: “the checker is unavailable” is an answer about the
verification process. It is not an answer that the proposed mathematical witness is false. The
retained attempt can later be replayed against the same protocol; testing the witness after a port
is added requires a new, explicitly versioned evaluation.
6. Design for a prespecified effectiveness experiment
The experiment is conditional on implementing and independently validating the three route
adapters. The existing manifest remains proposed_not_executed, with live execution disabled and
provider/model fields unset. The following design must be frozen before collecting confirmatory
data.
Use a finite task bank stratified by route, diﬀiculty, satisfiable versus obstructed outcome, and
vocabulary relabeling. Reserve task families and a second model for held-out evaluation. Discovery
examples, admission examples, pilot examples, and confirmatory examples must be disjoint at the
underlying instance level. Surface renaming alone is insuﬀicient to make a new task.
The six planned arms are plain proposal (P), typed protocol (T), structured coverage guidance (C),
generic reflection (R), admitted detector policy with feedback (D), and solution-bearing assistance
(A). Every arm uses the same output schema, route checker, stopping budget, and task inputs. Arm
A is an assisted control and is excluded from claims about unassisted solution finding. The principal
contrast is D minus R: the combined detector policy and its feedback, relative to generic reflection.
Randomize arm order within fixture/model blocks and record the assignment. Model revisions,
provider route, temperature, seed support, prompt templates, detector bank, checker bytes, retry
policy, and budgets must be pinned. Unsupported seeds are reported as unsupported; repeated
7

--- PAGE 8 ---
requests then remain stochastic trials. The pilot estimates resource requirements and outcome
variation. It does not determine success thresholds after seeing the confirmatory result.
The primary effectiveness outcome is a run-level binary variable: an independently verified route
witness obtained within the common budget. Unknown, refused, timed-out, and exhausted runs are
retained in the denominator and reported separately. Verification errors are not silently converted
into incorrect model answers. A checker defect discovered during collection stops the affected
analysis and requires a new source-bound run.
The confirmatory analysis should estimate the D–R difference using fixture blocks as the depen-
dence unit. A prespecified paired or randomization analysis must respect the actual allocation.
Report route strata, model strata, the difference, and its uncertainty. The effectiveness gate is a
strictly positive lower endpoint of a 95% interval for the principal held-out comparison. Exploratory
comparisons are labeled exploratory, with their multiplicity reported.
7. Safety, transfer, and stopping criteria
False suppression is evaluated per independently valid, detector-eligible proposal. In D, log proposals
blocked by the enforcement policy and evaluate them with the same route oracle under a safe shadow
path. In R, run the frozen detector in shadow mode on observed proposals without changing
feedback. Record eligibility and whether the independent oracle accepts; do not infer validity from
a model’s declaration.
For every route proposed for use, require an upper 95% confidence bound below 1% on false suppres-
sion and no false terminal certificate. A route without enough valid eligible proposals is unassessed.
Effectiveness on one route cannot substitute for safety on another. Adversarial near misses supple-
ment observed proposal streams but must have separate denominators.
The sample-size issue is concrete. Under independent Bernoulli trials, observing zero failures in n
opportunities gives the one-sided exact upper bound 1 - 0.05^(1/n) , obtained by solving (1-p)^
n = 0.05 . Eight zero-failure observations leave a bound near 31%; 299 independent opportunities
are needed to bring it below 1%. Shared fixtures and correlated proposals invalidate a naive inde-
pendence assumption, so these numbers are an illustration of the planning burden, not this study’s
achieved safety result.
Held-out transfer evaluates the frozen detector bank without editing its rules. Report failures caused
by new task families and model behavior as well as successes. If a new detector is added, restart
the relevant held-out assessment. Stop a run on its declared call, token, time, or monetary budget.
Stop the study on a false terminal, compromised source binding, or loss of required custody records.
Budget exhaustion remains an observed outcome.

9. Threat model and limitations
An untrusted proposer may supply a verification flag, reuse a stale witness, name nonexistent
coverage, or exploit a missing adapter. An erroneous detector may reject valid novelty. A defective
host may accept a malformed transition. The protocol addresses these threats through typed inputs,
negative examples, independent route outcomes, and recorded refusals, each within its tested scope.
It does not assume that all host code is correct solely because it is outside the model. The canonical
route seam is currently missing; signal evidence is supplied to the filter; the self panel has limited
diversity; the four phrase detectors are unadmitted and cannot see negation or context; and illus-
trative package tests are not integration evidence. Formal models of fuel or authority apply only
to their stated types and do not prove correspondence to the Python host.

Appendix A. Two signals, one detector
Section 3 describes two safeguards. A pattern match (Signal 1) should refuse a proposal only when
a structural defect (Signal 2) corroborates it, and a candidate detector should be screened against
a self panel of strings treated as valid. Each safeguard rests on an assumption that is easy to lose
in an implementation. This appendix states three small lemmas that make the assumptions exact,
checks each one exhaustively on finite domains, audits the repository against them, and describes
one component that realizes the admission record of Section 3.
The checks are in code/detector_lemmas.py and code/tests/test_two_signals_one_detector.py.
They use exact integers and fractions, refuse floats and Booleans, and import nothing from the
repository. Unless stated otherwise, repository paths in this appendix refer to commit 30609de2c3
928238715375a9bac608256fa381d8, the revision audited on 2026-09-28, not to the code baseline of
the rest of the paper.

Write 𝑚 for the number of Signal 1 matches and 𝑠 for the Signal 2 record, which counts as present

A.2 Lemma A: a derived second signal collapses the table
Lemma A. Suppose Signal 2 is computed from Signal 1, 𝑠 = 𝑔(𝑚) for some function 𝑔. Then:
1. the decision is a function of 𝑚 alone, so two proposals with the same number of matches
receive the same decision whatever their structural defects;
2. DIAGNOSTIC_ONLY is reachable exactly when, for some 𝑚 ≥ 1 , 𝑔(𝑚) has no positive defect, or
𝑔(0) is a positive defect of low or medium severity;
3. if Signal 2 is present exactly when Signal 1 is, with one severity 𝜎 for every 𝑚 ≥ 1 , then
exactly two decisions are reachable: TOLERATED and QUARANTINED when 𝜎 is high or critical,
and TOLERATED and DEFENSIVE_LOCKDOWN otherwise.

Proof. Part 1 is substitution. For part 2, the table yields DIAGNOSTIC_ONLY only in the cell where

1. For 0 < 𝑡 < 1 the gate activates exactly when 𝜃 ≥ 𝜃 ∗ = 𝐾 (𝑡/(1 − 𝑡)) 1/𝑛.
2. At 𝑡 = 1/2 , 𝜃∗ = 𝐾 for every 𝑛, so the Hill coeﬀicient does not affect the decision. For other
𝑡, 𝑛 only moves the threshold: the gate with parameters (𝑛, 𝐾, 𝑡) decides exactly as the gate
with parameters (1, 𝜃∗, 1/2).

A.4 Lemma C: negative selection screens what it screens
A detector rule has a screened predicate 𝑃 , which the screen tests against the self panel 𝑆, and an
executed predicate 𝐸, which the matcher computes on the content it inspects. The screen admits
the rule when no 𝑥 ∈ 𝑆 satisfies 𝑃 . Screening is sound for 𝑆 if an admitted rule never fires on a
member of 𝑆.

Lemma C. Over a universe 𝑈 of inputs:
1. screening is sound for every finite panel 𝑆 ⊆ 𝑈 exactly when 𝐸(𝑥) implies 𝑃 (𝑥) for every
𝑥 ∈ 𝑈 ;
2. screening never rejects a rule that would not fire on the panel exactly when 𝑃 (𝑥) implies 𝐸(𝑥)
for every 𝑥 ∈ 𝑈 ;
3. both hold for every panel exactly when 𝐸 = 𝑃 on 𝑈 .
Proof. For part 1, if 𝐸 implies 𝑃 and the rule is admitted, no member of 𝑆 satisfies 𝑃 , so none
satisfies 𝐸. Conversely, if some 𝑥 has 𝐸(𝑥) but not 𝑃 (𝑥), the panel 𝑆 = {𝑥} admits the rule, which
then fires on 𝑥. Part 2 is the same argument with 𝐸 and 𝑃 exchanged, and part 3 combines the
two. □

A.6 A realized admission record: anti-skill V2
Section 3 asks that a detector be admitted only with a reproduced defect, valid near misses, a
frozen predicate and bound digests. One component of the repository states that record as types:
anti-skill V2, in omega_engine_workflows_agents_skills/domain/anti_skill/anti_skill_v2.py
and application/anti_skill/anti_skill_v2.py of the same package.
• A closed predicate language. A detector is an expression of at most 16 atoms, combined by ALL
or ANY. Each atom applies one of four operators ( EQUAL_FIELDS, NOT_SUBSET_FIELDS, VALUE_IN
, IS_EMPTY) to eight declared fields of a subject record. There is no free-form code, regular
expression or model verdict, so the predicate that is evaluated is the predicate that is recorded:
the condition 𝐸 = 𝑃 of Lemma C holds by construction.
• Positive and negative controls in at least two contexts. Evaluation refuses a specification whose
fixture bank lacks an expected hit, lacks a negative control, or draws its cases from fewer than
two contexts (application module, lines 140 to 148). A negative control cannot expect a hit,
and copied subjects are refused.
• Derived receipts. An evaluation receipt is refused unless its outcome equals the outcome
computed from its case results, and every record’s identifier is recomputed from its canonical
content.
• A human decision. Admission requires a closed APPROVE or REJECT decision bound to the
specification digest and to the evaluation receipt, and a verified registry requires one approving
decision per specification, matched one to one with a passing receipt.
At the audited commit three specifications, their receipts and three approving decisions exist in
a registry scoped to one engine profile (claim ledger row P3-26 gives the paths). The Python
admission verifier and the loader that would activate that registry are retired and return a typed
refusal (omega_engine_workflows_agents_skills/application/anti_skill_admission/verifier.py,
lines 25 to 45), no Rust code reads the registry, and the registry that the prompt reviewer loads is
empty. No detector is therefore admitted and active through this record. It shows that the admission
record of Section 3 can be written as types whose outcomes are derived rather than declared; whether
admitted detectors help remains the experiment of Sections 6 and 7. The corrections of 2026-09-29
leave anti-skill V2 unchanged; moving it into the immune-system package, where the corrected gate
and screen now live, is recorded as open work.
