Abstract
This monograph develops a finite ℓ1 obstruction framework for coboundary defects on discrete and presheaf
structures. Its executable core is an exact rational checker for supplied ℓ1 primal–dual KKT certificates on
finite graphs, supported by the mechanized integer-list lower-bound inequality general_finite_certificate_-
optimality in Lean 4. Several central mathematical results are established as rigorous deductive proofs in the
text (Theorem 2.1 on commuting contractive aggregation, Theorem 4.2 on the finite scalar KKT certificate,
Theorem 5.1 on the contractive-path endpoint bound, and Proposition E.7e on the comparison-operator
secular root), separate from the scoped formalization. The general 𝐿𝑝 and Schatten- 𝑝 discussions provide
mathematical context and numerical diagnostics; unrestricted matrix Schatten-1 trace norms are convex
but nonpolyhedral. A formerly proposed three-step local update is withdrawn: it admits non-optimal fixed
points and provides neither a descent theorem nor an operational solver.
Why this matters. Optimization over discrete cochain complexes provides computable certificates for
inconsistency and topological obstruction in distributed systems, applied topology, and evaluation networks.
When defect fields are observed through contractive measurements, local consistency does not imply global
exactness unless watcher kernels intersect the coboundary image trivially. This framework supplies exact
finite primal and dual certificates that characterize minimal quotient representatives and quantify topological
obstruction mass without relying on ungrounded asymptotic or continuous assumptions.

1.1 F oundations and Preceding Results
The study of coboundary defects on discrete and presheaf structures addresses a foundational question:
when local consistency conditions fail to glue into a global section, how can the resulting obstruction be
quantified, certified, and minimized? Preceding work establishes that under natural observer axioms, the ℓ1
norm emerges as the canonical diagnostic metric at four levels of structure:
1. Combinatorial: On finite graph cochains, edge locality, coordinate additivity over disjoint supports,
coordinate permutation symmetry, and graph isomorphism invariance uniquely select an ℓ1 diagnostic
geometry; non-uniform edge weights remain unless edge-uniformity is explicitly hypothesized.
2. F unctional-analytic: On presheaves of Banach spaces, edge-wise additivity, faithfulness, functoriality
under bounded linear endomorphisms, and isomorphism invariance select a weighted ℓ1 coboundary norm,
with scalar uniqueness established once edge weights are normalized.
3. Dynamical: Projection of linear state evolutions onto nonlinear constraint manifolds produces cobound-
ary defects that obstruct aﬀine linearity of the projected flow, are measured by the ℓ1 observer diagnostic,
and persist at non-vanishing density over finite time intervals independent of system size.
4. Cohomological: The ℓ1 geometry on 1-cochains induces the standard quotient norm Φ on cohomology
𝐻1 and the defect quotient 𝑄1 = 𝐶 1/im(𝑑0). In contrast to the ℓ2 Hodge decomposition—which can
inflate the sum of the ℓ1-costs of its components by a factor approaching 3 on cycle graphs—the ℓ1
quotient norm attains the exact minimal obstruction mass.
This monograph synthesizes these perspectives into a unified categorical and variational framework, extend-
ing the theory to operator-valued presheaves, contractive aggregation functors, and primal-dual algorithms.

Standing Non-Claims
1. No Physical Derivations: This work does not derive quantum mechanics, the Born rule, spacetime
geometry, Lorentz invariance, Yang–Mills field dynamics, or the Standard Model gauge group.
2. Discrete vs. Continuum: The rigorous results of this paper hold for finite graphs, finite posets, and
finite-dimensional operator algebras. Continuum limits (such as total-variation flows and mean-curvature
motion) and geometric emergence are formal analogies and remain open mathematical problems.
3. Withdrawn Primal-Dual Candidate Update: The discrete three-step primal–dual candidate update
(historical Definition 4.1 and Proposition 4.1) is refuted and withdrawn; it admits non-optimal fixed points
(e.g. on a single edge with 𝜔 = 1, 𝜎 = 1, 𝜏 = 1/2 ) and is not a valid descent or minimization algorithm.
No descent, convergence, or fixed-point characterization is claimed for it. The only optimality result
retained in this paper is the independent finite ℓ1 KKT certificate verification (Theorem 4.2), which
checks independently supplied primal-dual pairs via exact linear programming duality.
