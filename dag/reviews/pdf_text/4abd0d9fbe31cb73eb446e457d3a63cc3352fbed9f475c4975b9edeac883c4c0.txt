Observer-relative control of cochain residuals
Jeremy H. Carroll
2026-10-23
V ersion 2.1.3
Abstract
We study finite candidate-selection policies constrained by several coordinate observers. Each observer specifies a pro-
jection, positive weights, a space of removable differences and a closure map. The policy bounds the observer-relative
quotient residual, the closure residual and their ratio. We distinguish two coordinate estimates: a weighted norm bounds
the coordinates of its input, whereas a quotient seminorm bounds coordinates only of a selected minimizing residual. Com-
plete coordinate coverage does not remove this distinction. A one-edge example has zero quotient residual and arbitrarily
large raw coordinates, even with complete observation. We give the corrected estimates, counterexamples to stronger trans-
fer claims and a finite ranking rule. The supplied Lean development treats integer weighted norms; a numerical reference
implementation additionally evaluates projected quotient problems.
1. Introduction
Several observation maps can impose simultaneous constraints on a proposed repair. Such constraints are relative to the
declared maps: they do not establish stability under all possible measurements or transformations. The mathematical
question is what these inequalities actually control.
Finite-dimensional quotient norms and linear-program duality are standard [1]. The cochain condition used for closure
residuals is standard as well [2]. Our purpose is to specify a policy and identify the distinction between coordinate coverage,
residual bounds and bounds on the original field. In particular, a bound on distance to a subspace is not a bound on the
vector itself.

5. Transfer examples and candidate ranking
Complete coverage does not prohibit transfer between observed components. On two disjoint cycle coordinates with
weights (2, 1), changing (1, 0)to (0, 1)reduces the weighted norm from two to one. Both coordinates are observed,
yet the defect changes location. To forbid that transfer, a policy must include appropriate componentwise or partition
constraints.
Coverage also matters for input norms. Observers that see only the first two of three coordinates assign zero observed
norm to (0, 0, 𝐾)for every 𝐾. No bound on the third coordinate follows from those observations.

6. Numerical and formal evidence
code/mod_observer_robust_control_core.py implements finite-observer comparisons and a floating-point linear program
when a matrix 𝐵𝑜 is supplied. This linear program is a numerical computation, not an exact rational solver. The numerical
observer object checks matrix shapes but does not independently certify 𝐷𝑜𝐵𝑜 = 0 ; that identity is a hypothesis of the
cochain interpretation and must be checked separately for supplied matrices. The numerical controller also permits its con-
figured comparison margin on ratio and absolute ceilings. To interpret those checks mathematically, use the corresponding
inflated ceilings or set the comparison margin to zero; the observer’s residual tolerance remains a separate parameter.
lean4/ObserverRobustControlCore.lean and lean4/JointDetectorAdequacy.lean verify statements about integer
vectors, natural-number weights and weighted input norms. Their coordinate estimate instantiates Proposition 4.3, not a
raw-coordinate theorem for a quotient linear program. The zero-weight entries in their finite examples represent omitted
coordinates. The supplied census records the actual declaration set and admitted kernel axioms.
The tests include the quotient/raw-coordinate counterexample and distinguish numerical LP observations from exact arith-
metic identities. A successful finite test run does not establish statements about undeclared observers.
7. Discussion
The policy controls precisely the diagnostics it compares. Quotient residuals ignore removable components; raw weighted
norms do not. Complete coordinate coverage helps obtain coordinate estimates for raw input norms, but it cannot bound
arbitrary components removed by quotient minimization. Neither condition prevents all transfers, constraint deletion or
divergence under an unbounded cumulative tolerance. Those properties require additional hypotheses and checks.
