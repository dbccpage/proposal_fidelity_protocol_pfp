Study guide: Paper 010
AuthorityEffect: None. Study first, attempt the quiz, then use the separate
instructor key . Definitions come from manuscript Section 2; pilot data and
limits come from Sections 3–6.
1. Validate the algebra
A finite table must be total and closed, have a two-sided identity and inverses,
and be associative for every triple. Names and values need their declared types.
The S3 construction uses (p q)(x)=p(q(x)); Q8 uses integer quaternions. No
floating-point tolerance enters these checks.
In Q8, i j (−i) (−j)=−1, while i(−i)j(−j)=1. The code labels 1 and −1 as e
and -e. A −1 square is non-flat even though −1 is central and self-inverse.
2. Keep the path convention visible
Plaquettes are (r,c), (r+1,c), (r+1,c+1), (r,c+1). The last two edges use inverses
of stored positive links. Reversal inverts the product. Moving the basepoint to
the next vertex conjugates by the inverse of the first edge. Adjacent gauge
factors cancel along a path, leaving g(start) hol(path) inverse(g(end)) .
A closed product is conjugated at its basepoint: identity status persists, but its
exact element can change.
Use a non-central S3 product of order three to detect reversal errors. The Q8
commutator −1 alone cannot distinguish its two orientations.
3. Local flatness and global gauge triviality
The declared lattice includes its plaquettes. An open rectangle has trivial funda-
mental group, so flatness lets a rooted spanning-tree gauge trivialize every edge.
On a torus, flatness leaves commuting cycle products A and B; both must be
identity for a trivializing gauge. A=−1, B=1 therefore gives FLAT_NONTRIVIAL.
The host accepts open dimensions at least 2 and torus dimensions at least 3.
Endpoint-only names cannot distinguish parallel torus edges at size 2. For
seam links a,b, only the seam corner can be non-flat, with product b a b￿¹ a￿¹.
Enumerating S3² and Q8² covers 36+64=100 connections in this family, not all
connections.
4. Optimization and proof boundary
Four identity links have identity product. Skipping their multiplications pre-
serves the complete check; every other plaquette is still tested. The number
skipped is gauge-dependent. Group validation takes O(|G|³); after validation,
the lattice pass and tree work take O(LM). A submitted path of k edges adds
O(k) work.

Theoretical minimum: Paper 010
AuthorityEffect: None. Read the fictional preface and study guide. Keep the
instructor key closed while answering. Use the manuscript’s vertex order and
group convention. In the Q8 code, e means 1 and -e means −1.
Each of 24 questions earns 3 points: result, calculation/evidence, and condi-

Level 1: Group data before a verdict
1. A Q8 square has ordered links i, j, −i, −j. Compute its product and give
a permutation of these factors that changes the product.
2. Why check associativity before using a supplied table? How many triples
occur for S3 and Q8? State the asymptotic validation cost.
3. A table has all the right row labels, but one product is a list rather than
an element name. May the host issue a verdict? Name the refusal type.
Level 2: Orientation, basepoint and gauge
4. Derive the effects of reversing a closed path and rotating its basepoint
from v0 to v1. Why does question 1’s Q8 example hide some orientation
mistakes?
5. The canonical witness of C017 pois_01 has product r2. Its reversed wit-
ness states r. Must the package accept it? What additional checks are
required?
6. Show cancellation in a gauge-transformed path product. Which aspects
of a closed holonomy persist, and which can change?
Level 3: Local and global flatness
7. Every plaquette of a valid open 2×3 lattice is flat. Explain why a spanning-
tree gauge trivializes every edge. State the host’s minimum open dimen-
sions.
8. A flat Q8 torus has A=−1 and B=1. Give its verdict and explain why
local flatness does not supply a globally trivial gauge.
9. A 3×4 Q8 torus has column seams a=i, row seams b=j and identity links
elsewhere. Compute b a b￿¹ a￿¹ at the seam corner. Is it flat?
Level 4: Optimization and formal scope
10. A trivial 4×5 torus has 20 skippable plaquettes. After a gauge differing at
neighbouring vertices, none is skippable. What changes and what persists?
11. Separate group-table validation cost from lattice checking. How does ver-
ifying a supplied loop depend on its length?

3. GroupTableError: a list is not a declared element and may be unhashable.
No verdict.
4. Reversal gives hol￿¹; rotation gives U01￿¹ hol U01. Central self-inverse −1
stays unchanged under either operation.
5. Accept if it is an actual plaquette, its listed-order product is r, and it is

6. Adjacent g(v)￿¹g(v) cancel, leaving endpoint factors. Identity status and
