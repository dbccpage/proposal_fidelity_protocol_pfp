Abstract
A proposed solution and a checked solution are different objects. The Proposal Fidelity Protocol
(PFP), developed in the repository under the legacy name Symbol Game, makes this difference
explicit: a proposer supplies a candidate, while a host checks a declared mathematical predicate
before changing accepted state. We develop a small, fully specified example using inconsistent dif-
ferences on a finite graph. Weighted absolute residuals measure the quality of a proposed potential;
a separate quotient minimum measures the inconsistency of the fixed edge data. We derive their
relationship, give matching primal and dual witnesses, prove the weighted cycle formula, and ex-
plain sparsity and uniqueness with their necessary hypotheses. We also show why strict rational
descent alone does not ensure termination. The contribution is a reproducible protocol and exposi-
tion of established finite optimization facts, with an explicit account of what the current software
returns. A paper-local Python reference builds and checks the full primal and dual certificate on
small graphs. The native Rust solver returns only a minimum and a minimizing potential after
bounded exhaustive search. No model accuracy improvement or general reasoning guarantee is
inferred.
1. A problem that a reader can check
Readers beginning with symbols and packets can first work through Paper 000, especially its typed-
prompt and returned-packet notebook. This paper strengthens that finite proposal/check distinc-
tion: a candidate potential has a checkable cost, while a separate lower-bound witness is needed to
certify that the cost is optimal.
Suppose three instruments report relative offsets. The first says that 𝑏 is one unit above 𝑎; the
second says that 𝑐 is one unit above 𝑏; the third says that 𝑎 is one unit above 𝑐. Walking around
the triangle would raise a level by three units and return to its starting point. These three reports
cannot all be differences of one assignment of levels.
Write the directed edges as 𝑎 → 𝑏, 𝑏 → 𝑐, 𝑐 → 𝑎 , the reports as 𝑐 = (1, 1, 1), and the penalties for
disagreeing with them as 𝑤 = (1, 2, 3). A proposal consists of three levels 𝑠 = (𝑠 𝑎, 𝑠𝑏, 𝑠𝑐). Its cost is
𝐷𝑐(𝑠) = |1 − (𝑠 𝑏 − 𝑠𝑎)| + 2|1 − (𝑠𝑐 − 𝑠𝑏)| + 3|1 − (𝑠𝑎 − 𝑠𝑐)|.
The all-zero proposal costs six. The proposal 𝑠 = (0, −2, −1) has residuals (3, 0, 0)and costs three.
A host can verify both calculations without trusting the proposer’s explanation. To establish that
three is the best possible value, however, it needs an additional argument. That argument will be
a circulation: one unit of flow around the cycle gives an independent lower bound of three.
This example has practical interpretations in clock offsets, relative rankings, and consistency checks
on a ledger. Such interpretations require a separate decision about what the numbers mean and

PFP uses this small problem to expose three distinct questions: whether a candidate is well formed,
whether it improves an accepted state, and whether it certifies a mathematical conclusion. A
language model, a search routine, or a person may generate the candidate. The checking rule is the

changes the question. We call it report replacement here. Its new quotient cost is zero by construc-
tion, but the original reports have not thereby become consistent. This distinction is essential when
importing the calculation into a scientific application.

5. From mathematics to a proposal protocol
A minimal PFP run pins the input graph, reports, penalties, allowed moves, checker version, and
fuel. It stores an accepted potential and a record of decisions. A candidate never edits accepted
state directly.
For this section we define a strictly improving admission rule: an admitted section update must
parse, cover the required carrier, and strictly reduce 𝐷𝑐 under the fixed reports. An executable that
allows stationary updates has a different declared admission rule; the strict-descent statement does
not describe that policy. A refused candidate leaves the accepted mathematical state unchanged.
A terminal exactness claim needs zero residuals; a terminal optimality claim needs the declared
optimization evidence, ideally the primal and dual certificate above. An obstruction is a legitimate
answer when supported by the appropriate witness.
Monotonicity applies to this fixed-data update rule. Adding an inconsistent report can raise the cost.
Restoring an earlier checkpoint can also restore a higher cost. Neither action should be included in
a theorem saying that every admitted action decreases one number. Changes of input or rollback
must remain visible in the record.
Strict descent over the rationals is not a termination argument. On a single edge with report zero
and unit penalty, fix 𝑠𝑎 = 0 and propose 𝑠𝑏 = 2 −𝑡. The costs decrease forever and never equal

zero. A practical run instead consumes one unit of finite fuel per attempted transition and stops at
certification or an explicit unfinished result. This bounds attempts; a separate timeout is needed
to bound an individual parser, solver, or external call.
Hash chaining helps detect changes relative to a retained trusted root. It does not prove a mathemat-
ical claim, make a log physically immutable, or authenticate a provider by itself. Local mathematical
acceptance confers no external execution permission; the repository records that policy separately
from the arithmetic result.
6. What the implementation actually supplies
The package contains two exact implementations of the graph problem and three small Lean files.
Their scopes differ.
The native solver is graph_phi1.rs. It parses exact rationals written as integers or fractions, and it
rejects decimal strings, duplicate vertices, unknown endpoints, self-edges, and nonpositive weights.
It enumerates spanning-forest potentials and compares their exact weighted costs. Search is capped
at 100,000 forests. Exceeding the cap returns EnumerationLimit; it does not return a partial search
as an optimum.
The spanning-forest construction is justified by the hyperplane arrangement 𝑐𝑒 − (𝐵𝑠)𝑒 = 0. After
removing constant-potential freedom, its normals span the component potential space. The convex
piecewise-linear objective has an optimum at an arrangement vertex, and independent zero-residual
edges at a vertex form a spanning forest. Enumerating these vertices therefore supplies a finite
exact algorithm. It can be exponential in graph size. The existence of polynomial LP algorithms
does not make this implementation polynomial.
The native response contains phi1, gauge, and forests_checked. It does not contain a dual circula-
tion. The compiled program prints one JSON object: status PASS with these three fields, or status
REFUSE with a reason, in which case it exits with status 2. The Python adapter passes exact
rationals to that executable. On a PASS object it accepts phi1 and gauge entries only as integer or
fraction strings, requires the gauge to name exactly the declared vertices, requires forests_checked
to be an integer from 1 through 100,000, rejects a negative phi1, and requires the reported poten-
tial’s weighted absolute cost to equal phi1. That cost check is consistency of the returned primal
pair. It is not a dual lower bound, and the native enumeration stays trusted. A JSON array, a
non-object gauge, a float or Boolean, a decimal string, a missing forest count, or a cost mismatch
raises NativeFunctionalProtocolError. A native refusal, timeout, or nonzero exit still raises its own
typed error. The adapter does not substitute another solver’s answer for any of those failures. It
finds the executable through an explicitly configured path or the crate’s own build directory. It
does not hash or pin the binary, so a reproducible source-to-binary build needs additional custody.
Only when no executable exists, and the caller has not required the native path, does the adapter
answer with the Python reference described next.
The paper-local Python reference computes the same minimum and also the certificate of Section
3. For each connected component it enumerates spanning trees, capped at 100,000 per component;
exceeding the cap raises SpanningTreeLimitExceeded instead of returning a partial minimum. Among
minimizing trees it builds a balanced circulation within the capacities. It returns the potential and
the circulation only after an independent verifier has checked exact types, coverage of the declared
vertices and edges, balance, capacities, and equality of the two objectives. The governor built on
this reference applies the strict-descent rule of Section 5: it admits a potential update only when
the cost strictly decreases, and it computes the minimum with the reference rather than the native

The local Lean files also have limited statements. OddCycleObstruction.lean formalizes that no
assignment of signs ±1 to the vertices of a triangle makes all three edge products equal to −1.
InvolutionLaws.lean formalizes that a configuration and its image under a supplied sign-reversing,
weight-preserving map have signed natural-number weights summing to zero. NonAuthority.lean
encodes a declared policy as a type with a single value, so its theorem holds by construction and
checks no runtime behavior. The files build with Lean 4.34.0-rc1. The axiom report lists propext
and Quot.sound for the pair-cancellation theorem and no axioms for the other two. None establishes
the general graph LP, a production runtime, or termination of arbitrary rational descent.

A minimal independent mathematical replay should check the triangle’s primal cost six and three,
dual value three, and exactness after changing the third report to −2. It should reject a dual flow
with one entry deleted, an over-capacity circulation, an unknown endpoint, and a zero penalty. A
tied uniform cycle should admit multiple optimal residuals; a rational descending sequence should
exhaust fuel rather than be called convergent. Demo 1 performs each of these checks in exact
rational arithmetic. The package figure fig1_descent_and_obstruction.png plots the triangle
descent and the single-edge sequence from exact governor runs.
The claim ledger separates prose proofs, executable checks, and open implementation obligations.
Historical receipts remain in results/, and the contract files of the certificate gate used by Demo 2
remain in specs/; their source bindings and dates must be checked before reuse. Signed cancellation
and the exact Fock example are developed in the companion paper PFP II (Carroll, 2026a; see
docs/references.md). This package retains the exact Fock checker and its tests.
8. Related work, contribution, and limits
Necula’s Proof-Carrying Code (Necula, 1997) already separates untrusted production from
consumer-side checking against a declared policy. PFP applies this established design idea to
proposed reasoning moves and finite optimization witnesses. It does not claim to originate
independent verification.
The LP duality used here is standard; Boyd and Vandenberghe’s Convex Optimization (Boyd and
Vandenberghe, 2004) supplies a systematic treatment of primal and dual bounds. Our contribution
is the graph-specific derivation, exact examples, and mapping to a bounded implementation.
The protocol is useful when a problem has an explicit checker and candidate search is less trust-
worthy than verification. Its conclusions are conditional on correct input translation, a correct
checker, preserved arithmetic assumptions, and reliable execution. A faulty checker can consis-
tently accept a wrong claim. A correct graph certificate says nothing about unmodeled physics or
the quality of natural-language reasoning. Establishing model benefit requires a controlled compar-
ison such as the one proposed, but not yet run, in the companion paper PFP III (Carroll, 2026b;
see docs/references.md).
