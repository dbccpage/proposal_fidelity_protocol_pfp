## 1. Claim Statement

Under the safety and stopping criteria of Paper 003 Section 7, evaluating the deployment safety of a detector bank requires independent measurement of false suppression rates and false terminal outcomes separately on each operational route $r$, wherein proposal validity is established by an independent external route oracle (rather than model self-assertion), demanding an upper 95% confidence bound below 1% on false suppression and zero false terminal certificates per route.

## 2. Context & Hypotheses

- **Paper 003 Section 7 ("Safety, transfer, and stopping criteria")**:

  - Requires shadow-mode evaluation with independent verification oracles.
  - Specifies stopping rules for safety violations and sample size requirements under independent trials.
- **Hypotheses**: None.
- **Obligations**: None.

## 3. Derivation / Evidence

### 3.1 Mathematical Definition of Route False Suppression
For a given execution route $r$, false suppression is the conditional probability that the frozen policy blocks a proposal given that the proposal is independently valid and eligible for detector inspection:
$$P(\text{block} \mid \text{valid, eligible proposal on route } r) = \frac{N_{\text{valid, eligible, blocked}}}{N_{\text{valid, eligible}}}$$

Operational rules governing this metric:
1. **Denominator restriction**: The denominator $N_{\text{valid, eligible}}$ includes only proposals that are both detector-eligible and verified as valid by the independent route oracle.
2. **Invalid proposals excluded from denominator**: Blocked proposals that fail independent verification represent legitimate rejections and do not enlarge the denominator.
3. **Shadow verification**: In Arm D, proposals blocked by the policy are logged and evaluated by the route oracle via an isolated shadow path. In Arm R, detectors execute in shadow mode without altering feedback, and all generated proposals are tested by the route oracle.
4. **No model self-attestation**: Proposal validity is never inferred from model declarations, self-reported confidence, or syntactic coherence.

### 3.2 Route Isolation and Multiplicity Non-Transfer
Safety properties cannot be transferred across routes:
- Measured safety on route $A$ (e.g. discrete cochain verification) cannot substitute for safety on route $B$ (e.g. graph invariant checks).
- Each route must independently establish an upper 95% confidence bound below 1% ($p_{\text{upper}} < 0.01$).
- A simultaneous safety assertion across multiple routes requires a prespecified family-wise error rate or multiplicity adjustment.
- Any route lacking sufficient verified proposals remains unassessed and unadmitted for production deployment.

### 3.3 Sample Size Requirements under Bernoulli Trials
Under the model of independent Bernoulli trials with zero observed false suppressions across $n$ eligible opportunities, the exact one-sided 95% upper confidence limit $p_{\text{upper}}$ satisfies:
$$(1 - p_{\text{upper}})^n = 0.05 \implies p_{\text{upper}} = 1 - 0.05^{1/n}$$

Numerical evaluations:
- For $n = 8$:
  $$p_{\text{upper}} = 1 - 0.05^{1/8} \approx 1 - 0.6877 = 0.3123 \quad (31.2\%)$$
  Eight zero-failure observations leave an upper bound of approximately 31.2%, failing the 1% safety threshold.
- To achieve $p_{\text{upper}} < 0.01$:
  $$n > \frac{\ln(0.05)}{\ln(0.99)} \approx \frac{-2.99573}{-0.01005} \approx 298.07 \implies n \ge 299$$
  At least 299 independent zero-failure trials are required to bring the upper bound below 1%.

Paper 003 Section 7 emphasizes that this calculation illustrates the planning burden under idealized independence; in practice, proposal correlations and shared prompts increase the required sample size.

### 3.4 Zero Tolerance for False Terminal Certificates
A false terminal outcome occurs when a host issues a terminal refusal certificate against a proposal that was valid. The safety protocol requires:
- Exact zero tolerance ($N_{\text{false\_terminal}} = 0$).
- Occurrence of a single false terminal certificate triggers immediate study cessation and requires an audit of the checker and detector rules.

## 4. Limits and Non-Claims

1. **Protocol Requirement**: This derivation documents the measurement criteria defined in Paper 003 Section 7; it does not claim that these bounds were achieved in Paper 003.
2. **Dependence in Practice**: The $n = 299$ sample size calculation assumes independent Bernoulli trials; correlated proposal streams invalidate naive independence and require cluster-adjusted estimation.
3. **Route Boundedness**: Passing the safety gate on one specific route provides no assurance regarding performance on distinct routes.

## 5. Claim Hygiene Summary

1. **What it supports**: Confirms that Paper 003 Section 7 defines route-specific false suppression and false terminal outcomes as requiring independent oracle verification, with an upper 95% bound below 1% and zero false terminals.
2. **What it does not support**: Does not support claims that Paper 003 established empirical safety or bound false suppression below 1% in practice.
3. **Narrowest defensible claim**: False suppression and false terminal outcomes per route require independent verification under the safety protocol of Paper 003 Section 7.
4. **Next test needed**: Constructing an automated audit pipeline that evaluates logged shadow-mode proposals against independent route oracles and calculates exact binomial confidence intervals.
