## 1. Claim Statement

For any $p \in (1, \infty)$ on a finite graph $G = (V, E)$ with strictly positive edge weights $\mathbf{w} \in (0, \infty)^E$ and any cochain $c \in C^1(G)$, the minimizer of the weighted $L^p$ quotient defect functional:
$$J_p(f) = \frac{1}{p} \|c - d_0 f\|_{p, \mathbf{w}}^p = \frac{1}{p} \sum_{e \in E} w_e |c_e - (d_0 f)_e|^p$$
is strictly unique on the gauge-fixed subspace $F = \{f \in C^0(G) : f(v_j) = 0 \text{ for one root vertex } v_j \text{ per component}\}$, yields a strictly unique optimal residual $r_p^* = c - d_0 f_p^*$, and satisfies the nonlinear graph $p$-Laplacian equation:
$$d_0^\top \left( \mathbf{w} \odot |r_p^*|^{p-2} r_p^* \right) = 0.$$

## 2. Context & Hypotheses

- **Graph Topology**: Finite 1-dimensional cell complex $G = (V, E)$.
- **Positive Weights**: Weights $w_e > 0$ on each edge $e \in E$.
- **Exponent Range**: $p \in (1, \infty)$, with conjugate exponent $q = \frac{p}{p-1} \in (1, \infty)$.
- **Residual Vector**: $r(f) = c - d_0 f \in C^1(G)$.
- **Hadamard Notation**: $\mathbf{w} \odot |r|^{p-2} r$ denotes the edge vector with components $w_e |r_e|^{p-2} r_e = w_e |r_e|^{p-1} \operatorname{sgn}(r_e)$ (evaluated as $0$ when $r_e = 0$).

## 3. Derivation / Evidence

### 3.1 Strict Convexity and Residual Uniqueness
1. For any $p \in (1, \infty)$, the scalar function $\phi_p(t) = \frac{1}{p} |t|^p$ is strictly convex on $\mathbb{R}$: its derivative $\phi_p'(t) = |t|^{p-1} \operatorname{sgn}(t)$ is strictly monotonically increasing.
2. Because each weight $w_e > 0$, the sum:
   $$R(r) = \frac{1}{p} \sum_{e \in E} w_e |r_e|^p$$
   is strictly convex on $\mathbb{R}^E$.
3. The affine subspace of residuals $\mathcal{A} = \{c - d_0 f : f \in C^0(G)\}$ is a convex, non-empty, finite-dimensional subspace.
4. Strict convexity ensures that $R(r)$ attains its minimum at a strictly unique residual vector $r_p^* \in \mathcal{A}$.

### 3.2 Gauge-Fixed Potential Uniqueness
1. Let $G$ have $k$ connected components. The kernel $\ker d_0$ consists of potentials that are constant on each component ($\dim \ker d_0 = k$).
2. The gauge-fixed subspace $F$ restricts potentials to have value $0$ at one selected root vertex per component.
3. The restricted linear map $d_0|_F: F \to C^1(G)$ is injective ($\ker(d_0|_F) = \{0\}$).
4. Because $d_0|_F$ is injective, the unique optimal residual $r_p^*$ corresponds to a strictly unique potential $f_p^* \in F$.

### 3.3 Continuous Differentiability and Euler-Lagrange Condition
1. For $p \in (1, \infty)$, the function $t \mapsto \frac{1}{p} |t|^p$ is continuously differentiable on all of $\mathbb{R}$ with derivative:
   $$\frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{1}{p} |t|^p \right) = |t|^{p-1} \operatorname{sgn}(t) = |t|^{p-2} t.$$
2. Applying the chain rule to $J_p(f)$:
   $$\frac{\partial J_p}{\partial f(u)} = -\sum_{e=(u, v)} w_e |r_e|^{p-2} r_e + \sum_{e=(v, u)} w_e |r_e|^{p-2} r_e = -\left( d_0^\top (\mathbf{w} \odot |r|^{p-2} r) \right)_u.$$
3. At an unconstrained minimizer $f_p^*$, the gradient $\nabla J_p(f_p^*)$ must vanish:
   $$\nabla J_p(f_p^*) = -d_0^\top \left( \mathbf{w} \odot |r_p^*|^{p-2} r_p^* \right) = 0.$$
4. This yields the discrete nonlinear graph $p$-Laplace equation:
   $$d_0^\top \left( \mathbf{w} \odot |r_p^*|^{p-2} r_p^* \right) = 0.$$

### 3.4 Dual Circulation Witness
1. When $r_p^* \ne 0$, define the normalized dual chain:
   $$z_p^* = \frac{\mathbf{w} \odot |r_p^*|^{p-2} r_p^*}{\|r_p^*\|_{p, \mathbf{w}}^{p-1}}.$$
2. By Euler-Lagrange stationarity, $d_0^\top z_p^* = 0$, establishing that $z_p^*$ is a divergence-free circulation.
3. Direct calculation confirms:
   $$\|z_p^*\|_{q, \mathbf{w}^*} = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \langle z_p^*, c \rangle = \|r_p^*\|_{p, \mathbf{w}},$$
   satisfying strong Fenchel-Rockafellar Hölder duality with zero duality gap.

## 4. Limits and Non-Claims

- **Strict Convexity Regime**: Applies to $p \in (1, \infty)$. At $p = 1$, strict convexity fails and minimizers form non-unique polyhedral faces. At $p = \infty$, the maximum norm is non-smooth.
- **Positive Weights**: Weights must be strictly positive ($w_e > 0$). Non-positive weights violate convexity and positive definiteness.
- **Gauge Independence**: While the residual $r_p^*$ is unique, potentials without gauge fixing differ by elements of $\ker d_0$.

## 5. Claim Hygiene Summary

1. **What it supports**:
   Supports strict uniqueness of the optimal residual $r_p^*$ and Euler-Lagrange stationarity $d_0^\top(\mathbf{w} \odot |r_p^*|^{p-2} r_p^*) = 0$ for $p \in (1, \infty)$ on finite graphs with positive weights.
2. **What it does not support**:
   Does not support uniqueness at $p = 1$ or $p = \infty$; does not assert gradient differentiability at non-smooth endpoints without subgradient formulations.
3. **Narrowest defensible claim**:
   For $p \in (1, \infty)$ and $w_e > 0$, the $L^p$ residual minimizer is strictly unique, and the Euler-Lagrange gradient condition $d_0^\top(\mathbf{w} \odot |r|^{p-2} r) = 0$ holds at optimality.
