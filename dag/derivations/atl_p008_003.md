## Claim

If $d\circ u=\mathrm{id}$ then $u$ is injective, but $u$ need not be a conservative extension.

## Hypotheses

- Maps $u:A\to B$, $d:B\to A$
- Theories with declared meanings and inference rules

## Derivation

$u(a)=u(a')\Rightarrow a=d(u(a))=d(u(a'))=a'$. Counterexample: let $B$ add an inference rule deriving a new old-language proposition $\varphi$ not derivable in $A$; $u,d$ on terms are unchanged so $d\circ u=\mathrm{id}$ still holds, yet $B\vdash\varphi$ and $A\nvdash\varphi$.
