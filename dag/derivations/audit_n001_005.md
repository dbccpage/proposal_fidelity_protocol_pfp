## 1. Claim Statement

For any finite graph $G = (V, E)$ with strictly positive edge weights $\mathbf{w} \in (0, \infty)^E$, any cochain $c \in C^1(G)$, and any exponent $p \in [1, \infty]$ with Hölder conjugate exponent $q \in [1, \infty]$ satisfying $\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1$, strong duality holds:
$$\Phi_p([c]) = \inf_{f \in C^0(G)} \|c - d_0 f\|_{p, \mathbf{w}} = \max_{y \in C_1(G)} \left\{ \langle y, c \rangle : d_0^* y = 0, \quad \|y\|_{q, \mathbf{w}^*} \le 1 \right\}.$$
Both the primal minimum and dual maximum are attained, with zero duality gap.

## 2. Context & Hypotheses

- **Finite Graph**: $G = (V, E)$ is a finite 1-dimensional cell complex with $|V|$ vertices and $|E|$ edges.
- **Strictly Positive Weights**: Each edge $e \in E$ has weight $w_e > 0$.
- **Conjugate Exponents**: Exponent pair $(p, q)$ satisfies $p, q \in [1, \infty]$ and $1/p + 1/q = 1$:
  - When $p = 1$, $q = \infty$.
  - When $p = 2$, $q = 2$.
  - When $p = \infty$, $q = 1$.
  - When $1 < p < \infty$, $q = \frac{p}{p-1} \in (1, \infty)$.
- **Primal Quotient Norm**:
  $$\Phi_p([c]) = \inf_{f \in C^0(G)} \|c - d_0 f\|_{p, \mathbf{w}},$$
  where $\|r\|_{p, \mathbf{w}} = \left( \sum_{e \in E} w_e |r_e|^p \right)^{1/p}$ for $p < \infty$, and $\|r\|_{\infty, \mathbf{w}} = \max_{e \in E} w_e |r_e|$.
- **Dual Norm on 1-Chains**:
  $$\|y\|_{q, \mathbf{w}^*} = \left( \sum_{e \in E} w_e^{1-q} |y_e|^q \right)^{1/q} \quad (1 < q < \infty),$$
  $$\|y\|_{\infty, \mathbf{w}^*} = \max_{e \in E} \frac{|y_e|}{w_e} \quad (q = \infty, \ p = 1),$$
  $$\|y\|_{1, \mathbf{w}^*} = \sum_{e \in E} \frac{|y_e|}{w_e} \quad (q = 1, \ p = \infty).$$
- **Divergence-Free Annihilator**:
  The condition $d_0^* y = 0$ specifies that $y \in \ker d_0^* = (\mathrm{im}\, d_0)^\perp$, meaning $y$ is a cycle circulation.

## 3. Derivation / Evidence

### 3.1 Hölder Weak Duality
1. Let $y \in C_1(G)$ satisfy the circulation condition $d_0^* y = 0$, and let $f \in C^0(G)$ be any potential.
2. By adjoint pairing:
   $$\langle y, d_0 f \rangle = \langle d_0^* y, f \rangle = \langle 0, f \rangle = 0.$$
3. Therefore, for any potential $f$:
   $$\langle y, c \rangle = \langle y, c - d_0 f \rangle = \sum_{e \in E} y_e (c_e - (d_0 f)_e).$$
4. Decomposing each term with edge weights:
   $$y_e (c_e - (d_0 f)_e) = \left( w_e^{-1/p} y_e \right) \cdot \left( w_e^{1/p} (c_e - (d_0 f)_e) \right).$$
5. Applying classical Hölder's inequality on $\mathbb{R}^E$ with exponents $(p, q)$:
   $$\sum_{e \in E} y_e (c_e - (d_0 f)_e) \le \left( \sum_{e \in E} (w_e^{-1/p} |y_e|)^q \right)^{1/q} \left( \sum_{e \in E} (w_e^{1/p} |c_e - (d_0 f)_e|)^p \right)^{1/p}.$$
6. Observing that $q/p = q(1 - 1/q) = q - 1$, so $w_e^{-q/p} = w_e^{1-q}$, the first factor is $\|y\|_{q, \mathbf{w}^*}$ and the second factor is $\|c - d_0 f\|_{p, \mathbf{w}}$.
7. Under the dual constraint $\|y\|_{q, \mathbf{w}^*} \le 1$:
   $$\langle y, c \rangle \le \|c - d_0 f\|_{p, \mathbf{w}}.$$
8. Taking the infimum over all $f \in C^0(G)$ on the right-hand side yields weak duality:
   $$\max_{y \in \ker d_0^*, \|y\|_{q, \mathbf{w}^*} \le 1} \langle y, c \rangle \le \Phi_p([c]).$$

### 3.2 Strong Duality via Finite-Dimensional Fenchel-Rockafellar Duality
1. Let $E = \mathbb{R}^m$ where $m = |E|$. Define the convex function $g: \mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R}$ by:
   $$g(u) = \|c - u\|_{p, \mathbf{w}}.$$
2. The primal problem is $\inf_{u \in L} g(u)$ where $L = \mathrm{im}\, d_0 \subset \mathbb{R}^m$ is a linear subspace.
3. The function $g$ is convex and continuous on all of $\mathbb{R}^m$, so its effective domain has non-empty interior $\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom} g) = \mathbb{R}^m$.
4. The subspace $L$ trivially intersects the interior of $\operatorname{dom} g$ (e.g. at $0$).
5. By the Fenchel-Rockafellar duality theorem in finite dimensions (Rockafellar 1970, Theorem 31.1), there is zero duality gap:
   $$\inf_{u \in L} g(u) = \max_{y \in L^\perp} \{ \langle y, c \rangle - g^*(y) \},$$
   and the dual maximum is attained.
6. The orthogonal complement $L^\perp = (\mathrm{im}\, d_0)^\perp = \ker d_0^*$.
7. The Fenchel conjugate of the shifted norm $u \mapsto \|c - u\|_{p, \mathbf{w}}$ is:
   $$g^*(y) = \sup_{u \in \mathbb{R}^m} \{ \langle y, u \rangle - \|c - u\|_{p, \mathbf{w}} \} = \langle y, c \rangle + \sup_{r \in \mathbb{R}^m} \{ \langle -y, r \rangle - \|r\|_{p, \mathbf{w}} \}.$$
8. By standard convex analysis, the conjugate of a norm $\|\cdot\|$ is the indicator function of the unit ball of the dual norm:
   $$\sup_{r} \{ \langle -y, r \rangle - \|r\|_{p, \mathbf{w}} \} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } \|y\|_{q, \mathbf{w}^*} \le 1, \\ +\infty & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$
9. Substituting into the Fenchel-Rockafellar equation:
   $$\inf_{f \in C^0(G)} \|c - d_0 f\|_{p, \mathbf{w}} = \max_{y \in \ker d_0^*, \|y\|_{q, \mathbf{w}^*} \le 1} \langle y, c \rangle.$$
10. Compactness of the dual feasible set $\ker d_0^* \cap \{y : \|y\|_{q, \mathbf{w}^*} \le 1\}$ in finite dimensions establishes attainment of the dual maximum.

### 3.3 Explicit Dual Attainment Witnesses
1. **$p = 1, q = \infty$ (Polyhedral regime)**:
   The problem is an exact linear program. At optimal primal residual $r^* = c - d_0 f^*$, the dual circulation $y^*$ satisfies $y_e^* = w_e \operatorname{sgn}(r_e^*)$ for active edges $r_e^* \ne 0$ and $|y_e^*| \le w_e$ for $r_e^* = 0$. Then $\|y^*\|_{\infty, \mathbf{w}^*} \le 1$ and $\langle y^*, c \rangle = \sum_e w_e |r_e^*| = \Phi_1([c])$.
2. **$p = 2, q = 2$ (Euclidean / Hilbert regime)**:
   The optimal residual $r^* = c - d_0 f^*$ is the orthogonal projection onto $(\mathrm{im}\, d_0)^\perp$ under the weighted inner product. Setting $y^* = W r^* / \|r^*\|_{2, \mathbf{w}}$ for $r^* \ne 0$ yields $d_0^* y^* = 0$, $\|y^*\|_{2, \mathbf{w}^*} = 1$, and $\langle y^*, c \rangle = \|r^*\|_{2, \mathbf{w}} = \Phi_2([c])$.
3. **$p = \infty, q = 1$ (Chebyshev regime)**:
   Dual circulation $y^*$ concentrates weight $\sum_e |y_e^*|/w_e = 1$ on the active bottleneck edges achieving maximal deviation $\|c - d_0 f^*\|_{\infty, \mathbf{w}}$.
4. **$1 < p < \infty$ (Uniformly convex regime)**:
   The objective is strictly convex on the gauge-fixed complement. The unique optimal residual $r^*$ defines dual witness $y_e^* = w_e |r_e^*|^{p-1} \operatorname{sgn}(r_e^*) / \|r^*\|_{p, \mathbf{w}}^{p-1}$, satisfying $d_0^* y^* = 0$, $\|y^*\|_{q, \mathbf{w}^*} = 1$, and $\langle y^*, c \rangle = \|r^*\|_{p, \mathbf{w}} = \Phi_p([c])$.

## 4. Limits and Non-Claims

- **Finite Dimensionality**: Strong duality with zero duality gap relies on the finite dimensionality of the graph complex, ensuring continuous norms have non-empty interior.
- **Positive Weights**: Weights must be strictly positive ($w_e > 0$). Vanishing weights allow non-trivial kernels in the dual norm.
- **Extends Prior Audit Scope**: Extends the $p=1$ weak duality bounds in ROUT-C008 and AC1120-C011 to strong duality across all $p \in [1, \infty]$.

## 5. Claim Hygiene Summary

1. **What it supports**:
   Supports the conclusion that on finite graphs with positive edge weights and conjugate exponents $1/p + 1/q = 1$, strong Fenchel-Rockafellar duality establishes $\Phi_p([c]) = \max \{ \langle y, c \rangle : d_0^* y = 0, \|y\|_{q, \mathbf{w}^*} \le 1 \}$ with zero duality gap and attained extrema.
2. **What it does not support**:
   Does not support zero duality gap on infinite-dimensional cell complexes without closed range hypotheses; does not apply to graphs with negative or vanishing weights.
3. **Narrowest defensible claim**:
   For finite graphs with positive edge weights and $p \in [1, \infty]$, the quotient norm $\Phi_p([c])$ equals the maximum dual circulation pairing $\langle y, c \rangle$ subject to $d_0^* y = 0$ and $\|y\|_{q, \mathbf{w}^*} \le 1$.
