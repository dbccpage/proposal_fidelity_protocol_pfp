## 1. Claim Statement

In the prespecified effectiveness experiment design of Paper 003 Section 6, the principal contrast evaluating failure detector intervention is arm D (admitted detector policy with feedback) minus arm R (generic reflection), measuring the joint effect of policy gating and tailored feedback content under matched stopping budgets, token accounting, and feedback opportunities.

## 2. Context & Hypotheses

- **Paper 003 Section 6 ("Design for a prespecified effectiveness experiment")**:
  - Defines a six-arm comparative experimental structure:
    1. **P**: Plain proposal (unprompted baseline).
    2. **T**: Typed protocol (structural schema constraints).
    3. **C**: Structured coverage guidance (coverage prompts).
    4. **R**: Generic reflection (reflection feedback without domain detector rules).
    5. **D**: Admitted detector policy with feedback (detector gate and specific diagnostic feedback).
    6. **A**: Solution-bearing assistance (assisted control providing direct solution hints).
- **Assisted Control Boundary**:
  - Arm A is excluded from claims regarding unassisted solution finding.
- **Hypotheses**: None.
- **Obligations**: None.

## 3. Derivation / Evidence

### 3.1 Definition of the Principal Contrast
Paper 003 Section 6 prespecifies that:
- The central evaluative contrast is the difference:
  $$\Delta_{\text{principal}} = D - R$$
- The comparison is conducted under strict experimental controls:
  1. Equal feedback opportunities across iterations.
  2. Equal computational, token, and stopping budgets.
  3. Identical task inputs, route checkers, and output schemas.
  4. Frozen differentiation of feedback content: Arm R provides non-specific generic reflection, whereas Arm D provides detector-governed diagnostics.

### 3.2 Joint Policy-plus-Feedback Estimand
The contrast $D - R$ does not measure the detector's isolated detection predicate in the abstract. Instead, it measures the combined operational intervention:
- Host policy gating (refusal and lockdown transitions).
- Structured diagnostic feedback emitted to the model on refusal.
Both elements operate together in deployment; separating feedback content from gate policy requires a separate factorial decomposition.

### 3.3 Prespecified Effectiveness Gate
The confirmatory decision gate in Paper 003 Section 6 requires:
- Primary outcome metric: run-level binary indicator of whether an independently verified route witness is obtained within the declared budget.
- Confirmatory gate: a strictly positive lower endpoint of a 95% confidence interval for the $D - R$ difference on held-out tasks:
  $$\text{LB}_{95}(D - R) > 0$$
- Incomplete, timed-out, refused, or budget-exhausted runs are retained in the denominator and reported in separate outcome categories.

### 3.4 Exclusion of Assisted Controls
Arm A includes solution-bearing hints that alter task solvability. It functions as an empirical upper bound on achievable performance but is explicitly barred from supporting claims of autonomous problem solving.

## 4. Limits and Non-Claims

1. **Design Statement**: This derivation documents the prespecified experimental protocol; Paper 003 marks the manifest as `proposed_not_executed` and presents no live causal effect estimate.
2. **Composite Estimand**: The contrast $D - R$ estimates the joint policy-and-feedback bundle and does not isolate the marginal contribution of the lexical pattern match.
3. **No Generalization Beyond Held-Out Tasks**: Effectiveness on tested task strata does not establish performance on tasks with unmodeled failure modes.

## 5. Claim Hygiene Summary

1. **What it supports**: Confirms that Paper 003 establishes $D - R$ as the principal prespecified contrast for evaluating detector policy with feedback against generic reflection.
2. **What it does not support**: Does not support claims of measured empirical superiority or observed performance gains for Arm D over Arm R.
3. **Narrowest defensible claim**: In the study design of Paper 003 Section 6, D versus R is the principal policy-plus-feedback comparison.
4. **Next test needed**: Executing a simulated or live randomized trial conforming to the Section 6 protocol and evaluating whether $\text{LB}_{95}(D - R) > 0$ holds.
