## 1. Claim Statement

Let $(P, \le)$ be a finite partially ordered set regarded as a small category whose objects are elements of $P$ and where a unique morphism exists from $x$ to $y$ if and only if $x \le y$. A contravariant functor
$$F: P^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Ban}$$
into the category of Banach spaces with bounded linear operators consists of:

1. A Banach space fiber $F(x)$ for each element $x \in P$.
2. A bounded linear restriction operator $r_{y, x} = F(x \le y): F(y) \to F(x)$ for each comparable pair $x \le y$, satisfying operator contractivity:
   $$\|r_{y, x}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le 1.$$
3. The identity law on diagonal elements:
   $$r_{x, x} = \mathrm{id}_{F(x)} \quad \text{for all } x \in P.$$
4. The chain composition law for any comparable triple $x \le y \le z$:
   $$r_{z, x} = r_{y, x} \circ r_{z, y}.$$

## 2. Context & Hypotheses

- **Finite Poset Carrier**: $(P, \le)$ is a finite partially ordered set satisfying reflexivity ($x \le x$), antisymmetry ($x \le y \land y \le x \implies x = y$), and transitivity ($x \le y \land y \le z \implies x \le z$).
- **Banach Stalk Spaces**: For each $x \in P$, $F(x)$ is a finite-dimensional or complete normed vector space over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ equipped with a norm $\|\cdot\|_{F(x)}$.
- **Bounded Operators**: Each restriction $r_{y, x}: F(y) \to F(x)$ is a linear map satisfying the operator norm bound:
  $$\|r_{y, x}\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \sup_{v \in F(y), \|v\| \le 1} \|r_{y, x}(v)\|_{F(x)} \le 1.$$
- **Presheaf Functoriality**: Contravariance reverses morphism arrows: the order relation $x \le y$ in $P$ induces the restriction map from upper stalk $F(y)$ down to lower stalk $F(x)$.

## 3. Derivation / Evidence

### 3.1 Functorial Axiom Verification

Functoriality follows directly from evaluating the two defining categorical conditions on the declared data:

1. **Identity Evaluation**:
   For any object $x \in P$, the identity morphism in $P^{\mathrm{op}}$ is the reverse of $\mathrm{id}_x: x \le x$. The functor preserves identity morphisms by declaration:
   $$F(\mathrm{id}_x) = \mathrm{id}_{F(x)} \implies r_{x, x} = \mathrm{id}_{F(x)}.$$
   In coordinates where $F(x) = \mathbb{R}^n$, the matrix representation of $r_{x, x}$ is the $n \times n$ identity matrix $I_n$.

2. **Composition Evaluation**:
   Let $x \le y \le z$ be a comparable triple in $P$. In the category $P$, the unique morphism $x \le z$ factors as the composite $(y \le z) \circ (x \le y)$. Under the contravariant functor $F$, morphism composition is reversed:
   $$F((y \le z) \circ (x \le y)) = F(x \le y) \circ F(y \le z).$$
   Substituting the restriction notation $r_{y, x} = F(x \le y)$ and $r_{z, y} = F(y \le z)$:
   $$r_{z, x} = r_{y, x} \circ r_{z, y}.$$
   This matches the chain composition law across the intermediate stalk $F(y)$.

3. **Submultiplicativity of Operator Norm**:
   Because each restriction map satisfies $\|r_{y, x}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le 1$ and $\|r_{z, y}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le 1$, the composite map satisfies:
   $$\|r_{z, x}\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \|r_{y, x} \circ r_{z, y}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \|r_{y, x}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \cdot \|r_{z, y}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le 1 \cdot 1 = 1.$$
   Thus, composite restriction operators automatically inherit contractivity from their factors.

## 4. Limits and Non-Claims

- **Finite Posets**: The definition applies to finite partially ordered sets; infinite or continuous posets require topological limits and uniform boundedness considerations.
- **Contractivity Convention**: Contractivity is assessed under the declared operator norm. Other norms on the underlying vector spaces may scale the numerical operator bound.
- **Strict Equality**: Functoriality here requires strict equality of maps ($r_{z, x} = r_{y, x} \circ r_{z, y}$), not weak equivalences or higher pseudofunctor coherences up to homotopy.

## 5. Claim Hygiene Summary

1. **What it supports**:
   Supports the structural consistency of contravariant Banach presheaves over finite posets under strict identity, composition, and contractivity laws.
2. **What it does not support**:
   Does not support weak pseudo-functors, non-linear maps, or infinite-dimensional topological sheaf cohomology.
3. **The narrowest defensible claim**:
   A declared assignment of Banach stalks and contractive linear maps over a finite poset forms a contravariant Banach presheaf if and only if it satisfies $r_{x, x} = \mathrm{id}_{F(x)}$ and $r_{z, x} = r_{y, x} \circ r_{z, y}$ for all $x \le y \le z$.
