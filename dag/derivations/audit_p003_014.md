## 1. Claim Statement

In the Paper 003 governance framework, admission of a failure detector into the host enforcement policy requires four mandatory artifacts: (1) a positive witness reproducing the named defect, (2) at least one independently valid near-miss control establishing specificity at the failure boundary, (3) a declared, bounded route scope, and (4) frozen cryptographic identities and fixture digests bound into the experiment manifest.

## 3. Derivation / Evidence

### 3.1 Structural Invariants of an Admitted Detector Record
Paper 003 Section 3 dictates that a detector admission record must contain:
1. **Identifier and version**: Unique, frozen string labels identifying the detection unit.
2. **Applicable route and input schema**: Declared operational domain restricting where the detector may execute.
3. **Executable predicate and defect code**: Formal predicate mapping candidate proposals to typed defect classifications.
4. **Discovery provenance and action policy**: Complete derivation trace and declared disposition (e.g., finding, hold, refusal).
5. **Positive defect witness**: Concrete reproduction demonstrating that the detector flags the targeted defect condition.
6. **Valid near-miss controls**: Independent valid instances close to the failure boundary (including alternative notations or relabelings) confirming specificity.
7. **Frozen digest bindings**: Cryptographic hash digests of detector predicate and test fixtures bound into the host manifest.

### 3.2 Five-Step Admission Procedure
Admission proceeds through five sequential validation steps:
1. **Defect Reproduction**: The host reparses the offending candidate proposal and verifies that the reported defect occurs.
2. **Example Minimization**: The failing example is minimized to isolate the precise failing condition and rule out incidental syntax.
3. **Near-Miss Construction**: Valid test cases adjacent to the failure boundary are authored and verified by an independent route oracle.
4. **Dual-Class Evaluation**: The detector is evaluated across both classes (positive defects and valid near misses) to confirm sensitivity and specificity, and its intended action policy is frozen.
5. **Manifest Binding**: The detector implementation, test fixtures, and assigned action are bound into the experiment manifest by cryptographic digest.

Any subsequent modification of the predicate or broadening of the route scope invalidates the admission record and requires a new evaluation cycle.

### 3.3 Rejection of Unsubstantiated Repetition Counting
Paper 003 Section 3 notes that counting raw string repetitions in an uncurated failure log is insufficient for admission:
- Repeated occurrences may stem from identical prompts, duplicate tasks, or checker bugs rather than independent failure instances.
- For example, the repository baseline self panel contains 1,000 recorded rows, but these decompose into only 40 distinct base contents (10 per category). That represents a finite regression corpus rather than evidence of detector generalization.

## 4. Limits and Non-Claims

1. **Protocol Specification Only**: This derivation formalizes the admission protocol specified in Paper 003; it does not claim that any candidate detector in Paper 003 has attained host admission.
2. **Bounded Specificity**: Validating near-miss fixtures does not eliminate false suppressions on unexamined open-domain inputs.
3. **No Automatic Repair**: Admission of a detector authorizes detection and declared policy actions, but does not grant authority for automated proposal mutation.

## 5. Claim Hygiene Summary

1. **What it supports**: Confirms that Paper 003 defines detector admission by the joint presence of reproduced defects, valid near-miss controls, declared route scope, and frozen manifest digests.
2. **What it does not support**: Does not support claims that candidate detectors in Paper 003 are admitted, or that meeting admission criteria eliminates runtime errors.
3. **Narrowest defensible claim**: Admission of a failure detector under Paper 003 requires a reproduced defect, valid near misses, a declared route scope, and frozen identities.
4. **Next test needed**: Implementing an automated gate that inspects detector registration records and refuses detectors missing any of the four mandatory admission artifacts.
