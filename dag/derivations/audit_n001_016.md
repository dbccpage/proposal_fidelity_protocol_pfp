## 1. Claim Statement

For any finite directed graph $G = (V, E)$ with integer cochain $\boldsymbol{\omega} \in \mathbb{Z}^E$ and strictly positive integer weights $\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}^E$, the $L^1$ quotient minimization problem:
$$\min_{f \in F} \|\boldsymbol{\omega} + d_0 f\|_{1, \mathbf{w}}$$
admits an optimal primal potential $\mathbf{f}^* \in \mathbb{Z}^V$ satisfying the gauge-fixing condition $f^*(v_0) = 0$ for a designated root vertex $v_0 \in V$, and an optimal residual vector $\mathbf{r}^* = \boldsymbol{\omega} + d_0 \mathbf{f}^* \in \mathbb{Z}^E$ whose components are all integers.

## 2. Context & Hypotheses

- **Graph Topology**: Finite directed 1-dimensional cell complex $G = (V, E)$ with $|V| = n$ and $|E| = m$.
- **Incidence Matrix**: $\mathbf{B} \in \{-1, 0, 1\}^{m \times n}$ where for edge $e = (u, v)$, $\mathbf{B}_{e, u} = -1$, $\mathbf{B}_{e, v} = 1$, and all other entries are zero.
- **Root Gauge Fixing**: Designation of root vertex $v_0 \in V$ and restriction to potentials $F = \{f \in \mathbb{R}^V : f(v_0) = 0\}$.
- **Reduced Incidence Matrix**: $\mathbf{B}_{\text{red}} \in \{-1, 0, 1\}^{m \times (n-1)}$ obtained by deleting the column corresponding to root vertex $v_0$.
- **Integer Data**: Cochain vector $\boldsymbol{\omega} \in \mathbb{Z}^E$ and weight vector $\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}^E$.

## 3. Derivation / Evidence

### 3.1 Total Unimodularity of the Reduced Incidence Matrix
1. A matrix $\mathbf{M}$ is totally unimodular (TU) if every square submatrix has determinant in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$.
2. The directed graph incidence matrix $\mathbf{B}$ satisfies the Poincaré property: every row (corresponding to an edge) contains at most one $+1$ and at most one $-1$, with all other entries equal to $0$.
3. Any submatrix of $\mathbf{B}$ inherits this property. Deleting the column corresponding to root vertex $v_0$ yields the reduced incidence matrix $\mathbf{B}_{\text{red}}$.
4. By the Hoffman-Kruskal theorem, any matrix where each row has at most one $+1$ and at most one $-1$ is totally unimodular. Hence, $\mathbf{B}_{\text{red}}$ is totally unimodular.

### 3.2 Polyhedral Formulation of the $L^1$ Quotient Problem
1. The $L^1$ quotient minimization problem can be written as a linear program in standard epigraph/split-variable form:
   $$\min_{\mathbf{r}^+, \mathbf{r}^-, \mathbf{f}} \sum_{e \in E} w_e (r_e^+ + r_e^-)$$
   subject to:
   $$\mathbf{r}^+ - \mathbf{r}^- - \mathbf{B}_{\text{red}} \mathbf{f} = \boldsymbol{\omega}, \quad \mathbf{r}^+ \ge \mathbf{0}, \quad \mathbf{r}^- \ge \mathbf{0}, \quad \mathbf{f} \in \mathbb{R}^{n-1}.$$
2. Decomposing the unrestricted potential $\mathbf{f} = \mathbf{f}^+ - \mathbf{f}^-$ with $\mathbf{f}^+, \mathbf{f}^- \ge \mathbf{0}$, the equality constraint matrix is:
   $$\mathbf{A} = \left[ \mathbf{I}_m \quad -\mathbf{I}_m \quad -\mathbf{B}_{\text{red}} \quad \mathbf{B}_{\text{red}} \right].$$
3. Standard operations that preserve total unimodularity include adjoining identity columns, duplicating columns with negated signs, and adjoining zero rows. Because $\mathbf{B}_{\text{red}}$ is totally unimodular, the augmented constraint matrix $\mathbf{A}$ is totally unimodular.

### 3.3 Extreme Point Integrality
1. Because weights $w_e > 0$ are strictly positive and the graph is finite, the objective is bounded below by $0$. Feasibility is guaranteed (for example, taking $\mathbf{f} = \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{r}^+ = \max(\boldsymbol{\omega}, \mathbf{0}), \mathbf{r}^- = \max(-\boldsymbol{\omega}, \mathbf{0})$).
2. By the Fundamental Theorem of Linear Programming, a linear program in standard form with a non-empty, bounded-below feasible polyhedron attains its minimum at an extreme point (basic feasible solution).
3. Let $\mathbf{x}^* = (\mathbf{r}^{+*}, \mathbf{r}^{-*}, \mathbf{f}^{+*}, \mathbf{f}^{-*})$ be an optimal basic feasible solution corresponding to basis matrix $\mathbf{A}_B$.
4. By Cramer's rule, each basic component satisfies:
   $$x_j^* = \frac{\det(\mathbf{A}_B^{(j)})}{\det(\mathbf{A}_B)},$$
   where $\mathbf{A}_B^{(j)}$ is obtained by replacing the $j$-th column with the right-hand side vector $\boldsymbol{\omega}$.
5. Because $\mathbf{A}$ is totally unimodular, $\det(\mathbf{A}_B) \in \{-1, 1\}$.
6. Because $\mathbf{A}$ is integral and $\boldsymbol{\omega} \in \mathbb{Z}^m$ is integral, $\det(\mathbf{A}_B^{(j)}) \in \mathbb{Z}$.
7. Therefore, every basic variable in $\mathbf{x}^*$ is an integer: $\mathbf{x}^* \in \mathbb{Z}^{2m + 2(n-1)}$.
8. Setting $\mathbf{f}^* = \mathbf{f}^{+*} - \mathbf{f}^{-*} \in \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}$ (and $f^*(v_0) = 0$), the potential is integer-valued.
9. The resulting residual $\mathbf{r}^* = \boldsymbol{\omega} + \mathbf{B}_{\text{red}} \mathbf{f}^*$ is an integer vector: $\mathbf{r}^* \in \mathbb{Z}^E$.

- `test_n001_016_integer_attainment_exact_simplex_positive`: runs the exact rational LP solver on triangle and 4-cycle instances with integer data and verifies that denominators of $f^*$ and $r^*$ equal 1.

## 4. Limits and Non-Claims

- **Discrete Graph Restriction**: Applies to 1-dimensional directed graph complexes where incidence matrices have at most two non-zero entries per row. It does not generalize to higher-dimensional cell complexes (e.g. 2-complexes or hypergraphs), where total unimodularity in general fails.
- **Integer Hypothesis**: Relies strictly on the integrality of $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ and $\mathbf{w}$. Fractional or irrational weights/cochains can produce fractional optima.
- **Cut-Lattice Distinction**: This is a cochain/cut-lattice attainment statement. It does not claim integrality for homologous chain problems with non-unimodular higher boundary operators.

## 5. Claim Hygiene Summary

1. **What it supports**:
   Supports existence of an integral optimal potential $\mathbf{f}^* \in \mathbb{Z}^V$ and integral optimal residual $\mathbf{r}^* \in \mathbb{Z}^E$ for the $L^1$ quotient problem on directed graphs with integer cochains, positive integer weights, and root gauge fixing.
2. **What it does not support**:
   Does not support integrality for arbitrary real/fractional cochains; does not assert total unimodularity for 2-complexes or general hypergraphs.
3. **Narrowest defensible claim**:
   Under integer edge cochains and integer positive weights on a finite directed graph, the extreme points of the $L^1$ quotient minimization polyhedron are integer-valued.
