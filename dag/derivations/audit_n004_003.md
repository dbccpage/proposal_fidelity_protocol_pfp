## 1. Claim Statement

Let $(P, \le)$ be a finite partially ordered set equipped with a strict functorial presheaf $F: P^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Ban}$ assigning Banach fibers $F(x)$ and linear restriction maps $r_{y, x}: F(y) \to F(x)$ satisfying $r_{x, x} = \mathrm{id}_{F(x)}$ and $r_{z, x} = r_{y, x} \circ r_{z, y}$ for all $x \le y \le z$.

For any elementary diamond subposet in $P$ consisting of a bottom element $a$, distinct intermediate elements $b$ and $d$, and a top element $c$ satisfying:
$$a < b < c \quad \text{and} \quad a < d < c \quad (b \ne d),$$
the two restriction composites from the top stalk $F(c)$ to the bottom stalk $F(a)$ around the left and right flanks agree identically:
$$r_{b, a} \circ r_{c, b} = r_{d, a} \circ r_{c, d}.$$
Equivalently, the diamond defect operator vanishes on all vectors $v \in F(c)$:
$$D_{c, (b, d), a}(v) = (r_{b, a} \circ r_{c, b} - r_{d, a} \circ r_{c, d})(v) = 0 \in F(a).$$

## 2. Context & Hypotheses

- **Elementary Diamond**: A 4-element subposet $\{a, b, d, c\} \subset P$ where $a$ is the infimum, $c$ is the supremum, and $b, d$ are incomparable intermediate elements ($b \not\le d$ and $d \not\le b$).
- **Functorial Presheaf**: The restriction family $r$ satisfies the chain composition law on every comparable triple in $P$.
- **Direct Restriction**: The poset relation $a \le c$ establishes the existence of a direct restriction operator $r_{c, a}: F(c) \to F(a)$.

## 3. Derivation / Evidence

### 3.1 Algebraic Derivation via Two-Fold Chain Composition

The commutativity of the diamond follows by two applications of the functorial chain composition law:

1. **Left Flank Evaluation**:
   Consider the comparable chain $a \le b \le c$. By the presheaf composition law:
   $$r_{c, a} = r_{b, a} \circ r_{c, b}.$$

2. **Right Flank Evaluation**:
   Consider the comparable chain $a \le d \le c$. By the presheaf composition law:
   $$r_{c, a} = r_{d, a} \circ r_{c, d}.$$

3. **Equality of Flank Composites**:
   Both the left composite $r_{b, a} \circ r_{c, b}$ and the right composite $r_{d, a} \circ r_{c, d}$ are equal to the same direct restriction operator $r_{c, a}: F(c) \to F(a)$.
   By symmetry and transitivity of equality in the space of bounded linear operators $\mathcal{B}(F(c), F(a))$:
   $$r_{b, a} \circ r_{c, b} = r_{d, a} \circ r_{c, d}.$$

4. **Defect Formulation**:
   Subtracting the right flank from the left flank yields:
   $$D_{c, (b, d), a} = r_{b, a} \circ r_{c, b} - r_{d, a} \circ r_{c, d} = r_{c, a} - r_{c, a} = 0.$$
   The diamond defect is the zero operator.

## 4. Limits and Non-Claims

- **Strict Functoriality Required**: Diamond path-independence holds for strict presheaves where composition is exact. In non-flat systems or higher gauge theories with non-trivial curvature $K \ne 0$, the two paths differ by a holonomy defect matrix.
- **Elementary Diamonds**: The claim treats 2-step paths across single intermediate nodes. Longer paths decompose into sequences of diamonds and chains in finite graded posets.

## 5. Claim Hygiene Summary

1. **What it supports**:
   Supports path-independence across elementary diamond subposets in any strict contravariant presheaf.
2. **What it does not support**:
   Does not support diamonds in curved or non-functorial restriction systems where intermediate composition defects are non-zero.
3. **The narrowest defensible claim**:
   In any contravariant presheaf satisfying chain composition, the composites along the two intermediate paths of an elementary diamond $a < b, d < c$ evaluate to the same linear operator $r_{c, a}$.
