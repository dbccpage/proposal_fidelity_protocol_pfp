## 1. Claim Statement

For arbitrary sets $A$ and $B$ and maps $u : A \to B$ and $d : B \to A \sqcup R$ satisfying the left-inverse identity $d(u(a)) = a$ for all $a \in A$, the map $u$ is injective on $A$. However, when $\mathcal{T}_A = (\mathcal{L}_A, \vdash_A)$ and $\mathcal{T}_B = (\mathcal{L}_B, \vdash_B)$ are deductive theories whose formula translation $u : \mathcal{L}_A \to \mathcal{L}_B$ admits a left inverse $d$ ($d \circ u = \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{L}_A}$), $(\mathcal{T}_B, u)$ need not be a conservative extension of $\mathcal{T}_A$: an inference rule in $\mathcal{T}_B$ can derive $u(\varphi)$ for an old-language proposition $\varphi \in \mathcal{L}_A$ that is not derivable in $\mathcal{T}_A$ ($\vdash_B u(\varphi)$ while $\nvdash_A \varphi$).

## 3. Derivation / Evidence

### 3.1 General Proposition: Left Inverse Implies Injectivity
Let $A$ and $B$ be sets, let $R$ be a disjoint refusal set ($A \cap R = \varnothing$), and let $u : A \to B$ and $d : B \to A \sqcup R$ satisfy:
$$\forall a \in A,\quad d(u(a)) = a.$$
Suppose $a, a' \in A$ satisfy $u(a) = u(a')$. Applying $d$ to the equal elements $u(a)$ and $u(a')$ in $B$ gives:
$$a = d(u(a)) = d(u(a')) = a'.$$
Therefore $u$ is injective on $A$ ($\forall a, a' \in A,\; u(a) = u(a') \implies a = a'$).

*Converse and out-of-image boundary*: For finite nonempty $A$ ($a_0 \in A$) and any injective $u : A \to B$, defining $d(b) = a$ when $b = u(a)$ (unique by injectivity) and $d(b) = a_0$ (if $R = \varnothing$) or $d(b) \in R$ (if $R \ne \varnothing$) when $b \notin u(A)$ satisfies $d \circ u = \mathrm{id}_A$. On $b \in B \setminus u(A)$, however, $d(u(a)) = a$ does not establish $u(d(b)) = b$: either $d(b) \in R$ lies outside the domain of $u$, or $u(d(b)) \in u(A)$ cannot equal $b \notin u(A)$.

### 3.2 Explicit Deductive Counterexample: Left Inverse Without Conservative Extension
A theory morphism $u : \mathcal{L}_A \to \mathcal{L}_B$ from $\mathcal{T}_A = (\mathcal{L}_A, \vdash_A)$ to $\mathcal{T}_B = (\mathcal{L}_B, \vdash_B)$ is a **conservative extension** if and only if:
$$\forall \chi \in \mathcal{L}_A,\quad \vdash_B u(\chi) \iff \vdash_A \chi.$$
In particular, conservativity requires reflection of old-language theorems: $\mathrm{Thm}(\mathcal{T}_B) \cap u(\mathcal{L}_A) \subseteq u(\mathrm{Thm}(\mathcal{T}_A))$.

Construct a concrete finite multi-element instance ($|\mathcal{L}_A| = 2$, so injectivity of $u$ on $\mathcal{L}_A$ is non-vacuous):
1. **Languages**:
   $$\mathcal{L}_A = \{\top_A, \varphi\} \quad (\top_A \ne \varphi),\qquad \mathcal{L}_B = \{\top_A, \varphi, \psi_{\text{new}}\} \quad (\psi_{\text{new}} \notin \mathcal{L}_A).$$
2. **Translation and Left Inverse**:
   Define $u : \mathcal{L}_A \to \mathcal{L}_B$ by $u(x) = x$ for $x \in \mathcal{L}_A$.
   Define $d_{\text{tot}} : \mathcal{L}_B \to \mathcal{L}_A$ by $d_{\text{tot}}(\top_A) = \top_A$, $d_{\text{tot}}(\varphi) = \varphi$, $d_{\text{tot}}(\psi_{\text{new}}) = \top_A$, and typed partial retraction $d_{\text{ref}} : \mathcal{L}_B \to \mathcal{L}_A \sqcup \{\mathsf{Refusal}\}$ by $d_{\text{ref}}(x) = x$ for $x \in \mathcal{L}_A$ and $d_{\text{ref}}(\psi_{\text{new}}) = \mathsf{Refusal}$.
   Both maps satisfy $d_{\text{tot}}(u(a)) = a$ and $d_{\text{ref}}(u(a)) = a$ for every $a \in \mathcal{L}_A$, so $d \circ u = \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{L}_A}$ holds and $u$ is injective.
3. **Base Theory $\mathcal{T}_A$**:
   Equip $\mathcal{T}_A$ with the single premise-free inference rule $R_A = \bigl\{(\varnothing, \top_A)\bigr\}$. Its deductive closure is:
   $$\mathrm{Thm}(\mathcal{T}_A) = \{\top_A\}, \qquad \varphi \notin \mathrm{Thm}(\mathcal{T}_A) \quad (\nvdash_A \varphi).$$
4. **Non-Conservative Extensions $\mathcal{T}_B^{(1)}$ and $\mathcal{T}_B^{(2)}$**:
   - *Direct old-proposition axiom rule* ($\mathcal{T}_B^{(1)}$, Paper 008 Section 3, lines 141–145): Let $R_B^{(1)} = R_A \cup \bigl\{(\varnothing, \varphi)\bigr\}$. Least-fixed-point closure yields $\mathrm{Thm}(\mathcal{T}_B^{(1)}) = \{\top_A, \varphi\}$.
   - *New-symbol mediated bridge rule* ($\mathcal{T}_B^{(2)}$): Let $R_B^{(2)} = R_A \cup \bigl\{(\varnothing, \psi_{\text{new}}),\; (\{\psi_{\text{new}}\}, \varphi)\bigr\}$. Least-fixed-point closure yields $\mathrm{Thm}(\mathcal{T}_B^{(2)}) = \{\top_A, \psi_{\text{new}}, \varphi\}$.
5. **Witness of Non-Conservativity**:
   In both $\mathcal{T}_B^{(1)}$ and $\mathcal{T}_B^{(2)}$, the old-language formula $\varphi \in \mathcal{L}_A$ satisfies:
   $$u(\varphi) = \varphi \in \mathrm{Thm}(\mathcal{T}_B) \quad (\vdash_B u(\varphi)), \qquad \text{whereas} \qquad \varphi \notin \mathrm{Thm}(\mathcal{T}_A) \quad (\nvdash_A \varphi).$$
   Thus $d \circ u = \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{L}_A}$ holds while conservative extension fails.

### 3.3 Semantic / Model-Theoretic Counterpart
Under Boolean valuations $v : \mathcal{L} \to \{0, 1\}$ where a valuation models $(\mathcal{L}, R)$ iff $(\forall p \in \Gamma,\, v(p) = 1) \implies v(c) = 1$ for every rule $(\Gamma, c) \in R$:
- The Models of $\mathcal{T}_A$ are $M_A = \bigl\{(v(\top_A)=1, v(\varphi)=0),\; (v(\top_A)=1, v(\varphi)=1)\bigr\}$, so $\mathcal{T}_A \not\models \varphi$ (witnessed by $v_0(\top_A)=1, v_0(\varphi)=0$).
- In $\mathcal{T}_B^{(2)}$, every model $w \in M_B$ must satisfy $w(\top_A) = 1$, $w(\psi_{\text{new}}) = 1$, and $w(\varphi) = 1$, so $\mathcal{T}_B^{(2)} \models u(\varphi)$ and no $w \in M_B$ restricts along $u$ to $v_0$ ($w \circ u \ne v_0$). Semantic conservativity therefore also fails despite $d \circ u = \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{L}_A}$.

## 4. Limits and Non-Claims

1. **Syntactic vs. Semantic Scope**: Proof-theoretic conservativity ($\vdash_B u(\chi) \implies \vdash_A \chi$) and model-theoretic conservativity ($\mathcal{T}_B \models u(\chi) \implies \mathcal{T}_A \models \chi$, or model-expansion surjectivity) require explicit specification of inference rules and semantic interpretations, respectively.
2. **Tower Implementation Boundary**: Neither this general proposition nor `PAPER008-SINGLETON-LEFT-INVERSE` constructs context maps $u_n, d_{n+1}$ beyond the archived MSS-0/MSS-1 boundary.

## 5. Claim Hygiene Summary

1. **What it supports**: Establishes for arbitrary sets that $d \circ u = \mathrm{id}_A$ implies injectivity of $u$, and exhibits finite deductive and Boolean-valuation counterexamples on multi-element languages where $d \circ u = \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{L}_A}$ holds while $\mathcal{T}_B$ derives (and entails) an old-language proposition $\varphi$ not derivable (or entailed) in $\mathcal{T}_A$.
2. **What it does not support**: Does not support inferring theory conservativity, behavioral equivalence, or semantic preservation from round-trip syntax checks or stage-index increments.
3. **Narrowest defensible claim**: Existence of a left inverse $d$ with $d \circ u = \mathrm{id}_A$ entails injectivity of $u$ on $A$, whereas conservative extension additionally requires reflection of old-language consequences ($\vdash_B u(\chi) \implies \vdash_A \chi$), which fails whenever extended rules derive a previously underivable old-language formula.
4. **Next test needed**: For any proposed stage transition $(u_n, d_{n+1})$ beyond MSS-1, declare explicit typing/inference rules at stages $n$ and $n+1$ and check consequence reflection alongside syntactic round-trip tests.
