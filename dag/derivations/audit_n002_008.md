## 1. Claim Statement

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a finite directed graph complex with strictly positive edge weights $\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}^E$. The quotient defect norm $\Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega) = \min_{f \in \mathbb{R}^V} \|\omega - d_0 f\|_{1, \mathbf{w}}$ satisfies the additivity condition
$$\Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1 + \omega_2) = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1) + \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2)$$
if and only if there exists a circulation vector $z \in Z_{\mathbf{w}} = \{z \in \mathbb{R}^E : d_0^* z = 0, |z_e| \le w_e\}$ that simultaneously maximizes both linear pairings:
$$\langle z, \omega_1 \rangle = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1) \quad \text{and} \quad \langle z, \omega_2 \rangle = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2).$$

## 2. Context & Hypotheses

- **Graph Complex**: Directed graph $G = (V, E)$ with incidence coboundary $d_0: \mathbb{R}^V \to \mathbb{R}^E$ and divergence $d_0^*: \mathbb{R}^E \to \mathbb{R}^V$.
- **Edge Weights**: $\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}^E$, defining the weighted $\ell^1$ norm $\|x\|_{1, \mathbf{w}} = \sum_{e \in E} w_e |x_e|$.
- **Dual Circulation Polytope**: $Z_{\mathbf{w}} = \{z \in \mathbb{R}^E : d_0^* z = 0, |z_e| \le w_e \text{ for all } e \in E\}$.
- **Strong Duality**: On a finite graph complex with positive weights, linear programming strong duality gives
  $$\Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega) = \min_{f \in \mathbb{R}^V} \|\omega - d_0 f\|_{1, \mathbf{w}} = \max_{z \in Z_{\mathbf{w}}} \langle z, \omega \rangle.$$

## 3. Derivation / Evidence

### 3.1 Sufficiency of a Common Dual Maximizer ($\Leftarrow$)
1. Suppose there exists $z^* \in Z_{\mathbf{w}}$ such that $\langle z^*, \omega_1 \rangle = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1)$ and $\langle z^*, \omega_2 \rangle = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2)$.
2. By duality, $\Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1 + \omega_2) = \max_{z \in Z_{\mathbf{w}}} \langle z, \omega_1 + \omega_2 \rangle \ge \langle z^*, \omega_1 + \omega_2 \rangle$.
3. By linearity of the inner product:
   $$\langle z^*, \omega_1 + \omega_2 \rangle = \langle z^*, \omega_1 \rangle + \langle z^*, \omega_2 \rangle = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1) + \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2).$$
   Therefore $\Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1 + \omega_2) \ge \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1) + \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2)$.
4. By subadditivity of the quotient norm (triangle inequality):
   $$\Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1 + \omega_2) \le \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1) + \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2).$$
5. Squeezing both inequalities establishes:
   $$\Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1 + \omega_2) = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1) + \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2).$$

### 3.2 Necessity of a Common Dual Maximizer ($\Rightarrow$)
1. Suppose $\Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1 + \omega_2) = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1) + \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2)$.
2. Because $Z_{\mathbf{w}}$ is compact and nonempty, choose an optimal dual maximizer $\hat{z} \in Z_{\mathbf{w}}$ for the combined cochain $\omega_1 + \omega_2$:
   $$\langle \hat{z}, \omega_1 + \omega_2 \rangle = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1 + \omega_2).$$
3. By weak duality, for any $z \in Z_{\mathbf{w}}$, $\langle z, \omega_1 \rangle \le \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1)$ and $\langle z, \omega_2 \rangle \le \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2)$.
4. Writing the total duality gap:
   $$0 = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1 + \omega_2) - \langle \hat{z}, \omega_1 + \omega_2 \rangle = [\Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1) - \langle \hat{z}, \omega_1 \rangle] + [\Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2) - \langle \hat{z}, \omega_2 \rangle].$$
5. Both bracketed terms are non-negative. A sum of non-negative terms vanishes if and only if each term vanishes:
   $$\Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1) - \langle \hat{z}, \omega_1 \rangle = 0 \implies \langle \hat{z}, \omega_1 \rangle = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1),$$
   $$\Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2) - \langle \hat{z}, \omega_2 \rangle = 0 \implies \langle \hat{z}, \omega_2 \rangle = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2).$$
6. Thus $\hat{z}$ is a common dual maximizer for $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ in $Z_{\mathbf{w}}$.

## 4. Limits and Non-Claims

- **Conflicting Cycle Orientations**: Additivity fails when cycles share edges with conflicting orientation demands. On such graphs, strict subadditivity holds.
- **Positive Weights**: Requires $w_e > 0$. If $w_e = 0$, capacity constraint $|z_e| \le 0$ forces $z_e = 0$, altering the circulation polytope.
- **Finite Graphs**: Relies on finite-dimensional LP strong duality.

## 5. Claim Hygiene Summary

1. **What it supports**:
   Supports the characterization that the $\ell^1$ quotient defect norm is additive on a sum of cochains if and only if they admit a common dual circulation maximizer in $Z_{\mathbf{w}}$.
2. **What it does not support**:
   Does not support unconditional cycle additivity; does not assert additivity when cycles share edges with opposing sign requirements.
3. **Narrowest defensible claim**:
   On a finite graph with weights $\mathbf{w} > 0$, $\Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1 + \omega_2) = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1) + \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2)$ iff $\exists z \in Z_{\mathbf{w}}$ such that $\langle z, \omega_1 \rangle = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_1)$ and $\langle z, \omega_2 \rangle = \Phi_{\mathbf{w}}(\omega_2)$.
