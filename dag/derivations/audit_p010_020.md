## 1. Claim Statement

Across the five retained C017 case files:
1. The four `S3`-labelled fixtures (`pois_01.json`, `base_01.json`, `base_02.json`, `base_03.json`) carry the identical 6-element `FiniteGroup` $G_{S_3,\text{case}}$ on `('e', 'r', 'r2', 's', 'sr', 'sr2')` (matching `make_s3()` in `reference_solver/groups.py`), and among all $6! = 720$ bijections $\varphi: G_{S_3,\text{case}} \to \text{symmetric\_group\_s3()}$ onto the permutation group on $\{0,1,2\}$ (`('012', '021', '102', '120', '201', '210')`), `6` satisfy $\varphi(xy) = \varphi(x)\varphi(y)$ for all $x, y \in G_{S_3,\text{case}}$ ($|\operatorname{Iso}(G_{S_3,\text{case}}, S_3)| = |\operatorname{Aut}(S_3)| = 6$).

The `Q8`-labelled fixture `base_13.json` carries a `FiniteGroup` $G_{Q_8,\text{case}}$ on `('e', '-e', 'i', '-i', 'j', '-j', 'k', '-k')` that satisfies exact value equality `G_{Q_8,\text{case}} == quaternion_group_q8()` under `FiniteGroup.__eq__` (`holonomy.py:138-145`, comparing `_elements`, `_identity`, and `as_table()` across all 64 Cayley products), while equality across all 8 inverses follows from two-sided inverse uniqueness in `_check_axioms` (`holonomy.py:87-97`) and explicit `{x: g.inv(x) for x in g.elements}` comparison (also matching `make_q8()` in `reference_solver/groups.py`), with $|\operatorname{Aut}(Q_8)| = 24$.

## 3. Derivation / Evidence

### 3.1 All Six Isomorphisms Between Case $S_3$ and `symmetric_group_s3()`
In `pois_01.json`, `base_01.json`, `base_02.json`, and `base_03.json`, `connection_from_case` constructs the same `FiniteGroup` $G_{S_3,\text{case}}$ on $E_{\text{case}} = (\text{'e'}, \text{'r'}, \text{'r2'}, \text{'s'}, \text{'sr'}, \text{'sr2'})$ generated by `make_s3()` (`reference_solver/groups.py:9-37`), where `'r' \cdot 's' = 'sr'` and `'s' \cdot 'r' = 'r2' \cdot 's' = 'sr2'`:
$$r^3 = e, \qquad s^2 = e, \qquad s \cdot r \cdot s = r^2 = r^{-1}, \qquad \text{'sr'} = r \cdot s, \qquad \text{'sr2'} = s \cdot r = r^2 \cdot s.$$
In `code/holonomy.py` (`symmetric_group_s3`, lines 150–171), elements of $S_3$ are one-line permutation strings $p = p(0)p(1)p(2)$ on `('012', '021', '102', '120', '201', '210')` multiplied by left-to-right composition $(p \cdot q)(i) = p(q(i))$. Element orders partition $S_3$ into:
- Identity of order $1$: `'012'`,
- Two 3-cycles of order $3$: `'120'` ($0 \mapsto 1 \mapsto 2 \mapsto 0$) and `'201'` ($0 \mapsto 2 \mapsto 1 \mapsto 0$, with `'120'^2 = '201'`),
- Three transpositions of order $2$: `'021'` (fixes $0$), `'102'` (fixes $2$), and `'210'` (fixes $1$).

Any group isomorphism $\varphi: G_{S_3,\text{case}} \to \text{symmetric\_group\_s3()}$ preserves element orders, so $\varphi(\text{'e'}) = \text{'012'}$, $\varphi(\text{'r'}) \in \{\text{'120'}, \text{'201'}\}$ ($2$ choices), and $\varphi(\text{'s'}) \in \{\text{'021'}, \text{'102'}, \text{'210'}\}$ ($3$ choices). Because every 3-cycle $c \in \{\text{'120'}, \text{'201'}\}$ and transposition $t \in \{\text{'021'}, \text{'102'}, \text{'210'}\}$ in $S_3$ satisfy $c^3 = \text{'012'}$, $t^2 = \text{'012'}$, and $tct = c^2$, each of the $2 \times 3 = 6$ generator pairs extends uniquely via $\varphi(\text{'r2'}) = \varphi(\text{'r'})^2$, $\varphi(\text{'sr'}) = \varphi(\text{'r'})\varphi(\text{'s'})$, and $\varphi(\text{'sr2'}) = \varphi(\text{'s'})\varphi(\text{'r'})$ to a bijection preserving all $6 \times 6 = 36$ products. Exhaustive evaluation over all $6! = 720$ permutations (`5! = 120` fixing `'e' -> '012'`) yields these `6` homomorphisms and `714` non-homomorphisms:

| $\#$ | $\varphi(\text{'e'})$ | $\varphi(\text{'r'})$ | $\varphi(\text{'r2'})$ | $\varphi(\text{'s'})$ | $\varphi(\text{'sr'})$ | $\varphi(\text{'sr2'})$ | Generator pair $(\varphi(\text{'r'}), \varphi(\text{'s'}))$ |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

### 3.2 Entrywise Equality of Case $Q_8$ with `quaternion_group_q8()` & Small-Group Discrimination
1. **Exact `Q8` Table Equality**: In `base_13.json`, `connection_from_case(base_13).group` has `name = 'Q8'`, `elements = ('e', '-e', 'i', '-i', 'j', '-j', 'k', '-k')`, `identity = 'e'`, and satisfies `case_q8 == quaternion_group_q8()` under `FiniteGroup.__eq__` (`holonomy.py:138-145`), which compares `_elements`, `_identity`, and `as_table()` across all $8 \times 8 = 64$ Cayley products ($i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = -e$, $ij = k = -ji$, $jk = i = -kj$, $ki = j = -ik$), while equality across all $8$ inverses (`e -> e`, `-e -> -e`, `x -> -x` for $x \in \{i, j, k\}$) follows from two-sided inverse uniqueness in `_check_axioms` (`holonomy.py:87-97`) and explicit `{x: g.inv(x) for x in g.elements}` comparison, matching `make_q8()` in `reference_solver/groups.py` (lines 40–83). Enumerating automorphisms of `Q8` gives $|\operatorname{Aut}(Q_8)| = 6 \times 4 = 24$ ($\cong S_4$: $6$ order-4 images for $i$ times $4$ order-4 images outside $\{\pm\varphi(i)\}$ for $j$).
2. **Reference Small-Group Separation ($C_2, C_3, V_4, C_6, S_3, D_4, Q_8$)**: Constructing exact `FiniteGroup` tables for $C_2$ (order 2, $|\operatorname{Aut}| = 1$), $C_3$ (order 3, $|\operatorname{Aut}| = 2$), Klein four-group $V_4 \cong C_2 \times C_2$ (order 4, $|\operatorname{Aut}| = 6$), cyclic $C_6$ (order 6, abelian, $|\operatorname{Aut}| = 2$), and dihedral $D_4$ (order 8, nonabelian, $|\operatorname{Aut}| = 8$) confirms:
   - $|\operatorname{Iso}(G_{S_3,\text{case}}, H)| = 0$ for every $H \in \{C_2, C_3, V_4, C_6, D_4, Q_8\}$ (separating nonabelian $S_3$ from abelian $C_6$ of the same order $6$),
   - $|\operatorname{Iso}(G_{Q_8,\text{case}}, H)| = 0$ for every $H \in \{C_2, C_3, V_4, C_6, S_3, D_4\}$ (separating $Q_8$, which has a unique element `-e` of order $2$ and six elements of order $4$, from nonabelian $D_4$ of the same order $8$, which has five elements of order $2$ and two elements of order $4$).

## 4. Limits and Non-Claims

1. **Complementary Scope**: Complements `PAPER010-DATA-FINITE-GROUP-AND-LATTICE-ADMISSION`, which already checks the group axioms on the five case fixtures.
2. **Retained Fixture Scope**: Group identification covers the five retained C017 case files (`S3` on four cases and `Q8` on `base_13`); other upstream C017 fixtures (such as `SL(2, \mathbb{F}_3)` of order 24 in `reference_solver/groups.py`) lie outside the retained five-case pilot subset.
3. **Isomorphism vs Presentation Equality on $S_3$**: While `base_13`'s `Q8` table is literally equal to `quaternion_group_q8()`, the four `S3` case files use generator labels `('e', 'r', 'r2', 's', 'sr', 'sr2')` whereas `symmetric_group_s3()` uses permutation strings `('012', ..., '210')`, so `S3` identification holds up to the 6 explicit isomorphisms rather than string-key equality.

## 5. Claim Hygiene Summary

1. **What it supports**: Confirms that all four `S3` case fixtures share one multiplication table admitting `6` group isomorphisms onto `symmetric_group_s3()`, that `base_13`'s `Q8` group equals `quaternion_group_q8()` entrywise (`24` automorphisms), and that both case groups have `0` isomorphisms to non-isomorphic small reference groups ($C_2, C_3, V_4, C_6, D_4$).
2. **What it does not support**: Does not assert string-key identity between case `S3` labels and `symmetric_group_s3()` labels, and does not extend beyond the five retained C017 case files.
3. **Narrowest defensible claim**: The `S3` multiplication table in `pois_01`, `base_01`, `base_02`, and `base_03` has `6` isomorphisms to `symmetric_group_s3()`, and the `Q8` multiplication table in `base_13` equals `quaternion_group_q8()`.
4. **Next test needed**: None for the five retained C017 case tables; group-table corruption and equinumerous non-isomorphic group substitution ($C_6$ for $S_3$, $D_4$ for $Q_8$) are tested as falsification controls in `tests/test_w5_w2_shard_d.py`.
