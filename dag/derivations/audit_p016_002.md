## 3. Step-by-Step Derivation

### Step 1 — Iterative signature splitting operator

Given any partition (equivalence relation) \(\sim_k\) on \(X\) with quotient map \(p_k : X \to X/{\sim_k}\), define for each \(x \in X\) its step-\(k\) signature

\[
\sigma_k(x) = \Big(p_k(x),\; \big(\mathrm{sig}_{k,a}(x)\big)_{a \in A}\Big), \qquad
\mathrm{sig}_{k,a}(x) =
\begin{cases}
\bot & \text{if } x \notin D_a, \\
(c_a(x),\, p_k(t_a(x))) & \text{if } x \in D_a.
\end{cases}
\]

Set \(x \sim_{k+1} y \iff \sigma_k(x) = \sigma_k(y)\). Because the first coordinate of \(\sigma_k(x)\) is \(p_k(x)\), \(\sim_{k+1}\) always refines \(\sim_k\), so the block counts \(b_k = |X/{\sim_k}|\) satisfy \(b_k \le b_{k+1}\).

### Step 2 — Termination within at most \(|X| - 1\) proper splits

Whenever \(\sim_{k+1} \ne \sim_k\) (a *proper split*), at least one \(\sim_k\)-block splits into two or more nonempty \(\sim_{k+1}\)-blocks while no distinct \(\sim_k\)-blocks merge, so \(b_{k+1} \ge b_k + 1\). Since \(X \ne \emptyset\) implies \(b_0 \ge 1\) and every block is a nonempty pairwise-disjoint subset of \(X\), any chain of \(m\) proper splits satisfies

\[
1 + m \le b_0 + m \le b_m \le |X| \implies m \le |X| - 1.
\]

Because \(A\) and \(X\) are finite and `Fraction` equality is exact, computing \(\sigma_k\) is finite at each step, and the loop in `refine(table, labels)` (`code/replay.py`, lines 5–13) halts as soon as \(b_{k+1} = b_k\) (at iteration \(K \le |X| - 1\)). At termination, \(\sim_{K+1} = \sim_K\), meaning equal \(\sim_K\)-classes have identical enablement, exact cost, and successor \(\sim_K\)-class for every \(a \in A\), plus identical \(q_0\) and terminal labels (since \(\sim_K\) refines \(\sim_0\)). Thus \(\sim_K\) satisfies `AUDIT-P016-001`'s exact-quotient criterion.

### Step 3 — Coarsest-refinement universality by induction on \(k\)

Let \(\approx\) be *any* stable partition refining \(\sim_0\). We show \(\approx\) refines \(\sim_k\) for all \(k \ge 0\) by induction on \(k\):
- **Base case \(k = 0\)**: \(\approx\) refines \(\sim_0\) by assumption.
- **Inductive step \(k \to k+1\)**: Suppose \(\approx\) refines \(\sim_k\), and let \(x \approx y\). Then \(x \sim_k y\) by the induction hypothesis, so \(p_k(x) = p_k(y)\). For each \(a \in A\):
  - Since \(\approx\) is stable, \(x \in D_a \iff y \in D_a\). If both are disabled, \(\mathrm{sig}_{k,a}(x) = \bot = \mathrm{sig}_{k,a}(y)\).
  - If both are enabled, stability of \(\approx\) gives \(c_a(x) = c_a(y)\) and \(t_a(x) \approx t_a(y)\). Applying the induction hypothesis to \((t_a(x), t_a(y))\) yields \(t_a(x) \sim_k t_a(y)\), i.e., \(p_k(t_a(x)) = p_k(t_a(y))\), so \(\mathrm{sig}_{k,a}(x) = \mathrm{sig}_{k,a}(y)\).
  Hence \(\sigma_k(x) = \sigma_k(y)\), i.e., \(x \sim_{k+1} y\).

Setting \(k = K\) shows every stable refinement \(\approx\) of \(\sim_0\) refines \(\sim_K\); since \(\sim_K\) is itself a stable refinement of \(\sim_0\), \(\sim_K\) is the unique coarsest stable refinement of \(q_0\).
