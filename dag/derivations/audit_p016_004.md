## 2. Hypotheses

1. Finite state carrier \(X\), finite action alphabet \(A\), terminal subset \(T_{\mathrm{term}} \subseteq X\), and partial deterministic transitions \(t_a : D_a \to X\).
2. Strictly positive rational step costs: \(c_a(x) \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}\) (\(c_a(x) > 0\)) for every \(a \in A\) and \(x \in D_a\).

## 3. Step-by-Step Derivation

### Step 1 — Loop erasure strictly reduces cost and length

Let \(w = (a_1, \dots, a_m) \in A^*\) be any legal resolving word from \(s_0 \in X\) with state trajectory \(s_0, s_1, \dots, s_m\) (\(s_k = t_{a_k}(s_{k-1})\), \(s_m \in T_{\mathrm{term}}\)). Suppose \(w\) is *not* a simple path, meaning there exist indices \(0 \le i < j \le m\) with \(s_i = s_j = u\). Decompose \(w = w_1 \cdot w_2 \cdot w_3\) where:
- \(w_1 = (a_1, \dots, a_i)\) transitions \(s_0 \to^* u\) with cost \(C_1 = \sum_{k=1}^i c_{a_k}(s_{k-1}) \ge 0\),
- \(w_2 = (a_{i+1}, \dots, a_j)\) is nonempty (\(j - i \ge 1\)) and transitions \(u \to^+ u\) with cost \(C_2 = \sum_{k=i+1}^j c_{a_k}(s_{k-1}) > 0\) (a nonempty sum of strictly positive rationals), and
- \(w_3 = (a_{j+1}, \dots, a_m)\) transitions \(u \to^* s_m \in T_{\mathrm{term}}\) with cost \(C_3 = \sum_{k=j+1}^m c_{a_k}(s_{k-1}) \ge 0\).

Because transitions are deterministic and depend only on the current state, splicing out \(w_2\) yields \(w' = w_1 \cdot w_3\), which legally transitions \(s_0 \to^* u \to^* s_m \in T_{\mathrm{term}}\) with length \(|w'| = m - (j - i) < m\) and cost

\[
C(s_0, w') = C_1 + C_3 < C_1 + C_2 + C_3 = C(s_0, w).
\]

### Step 2 — Existence of minimum-cost simple paths of length \(\le |X| - 1\)

1. **Simple-path length bound**: Any simple path \(s_0, s_1, \dots, s_m\) visits \(m + 1\) pairwise-distinct states in \(X\), so \(m + 1 \le |X|\), i.e., \(m \le |X| - 1\) (when \(X \ne \emptyset\)).
2. **Reduction to finite simple paths**: By Step 1, no non-simple resolving word can achieve the minimum cost (since erasing any repeated-state cycle produces a strictly cheaper resolving word), and iterating loop erasure at most \(|w|\) times reduces any resolving word \(w\) to a simple resolving word \(\tilde{w}\) with \(C(s_0, \tilde{w}) \le C(s_0, w)\) and \(|\tilde{w}| \le |X| - 1\).
3. **Attainment of minimum**: Because \(A\) and \(X\) are finite, the set \(S_{\mathrm{simple}}(s_0)\) of simple resolving words from \(s_0\) has cardinality at most \(\sum_{m=0}^{|X|-1} |A|^m < \infty\). Whenever at least one resolving word exists, \(S_{\mathrm{simple}}(s_0)\) is finite and nonempty, so \(\min_{w \in S_{\mathrm{simple}}(s_0)} C(s_0, w)\) is attained on a simple path of at most \(|X| - 1\) transitions and equals \(\inf_{w \in R_{\mathrm{res}}(s_0)} C(s_0, w)\).

## 5. Open Obligations & Scope Limits

- Strict positivity \(c_a(x) > 0\) and determinism are essential: zero-cost cycles preserve minimum cost without forcing strict simple-path uniqueness, and negative-cost cycles destroy finite attainment (`minimum_cost` explicitly raises `ValueError("positive operation costs required")` when any price \(\le 0\)).
