## 1. Claim Statement

For $p = 2$ on any finite graph $G = (V, E)$ with strictly positive diagonal edge weights $\mathbf{W} = \operatorname{diag}(w_e)$, the weighted least-squares potential is given by the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse:
$$f^* = \mathbf{L}^+ d_0^\top \mathbf{W} c,$$
where $\mathbf{L} = d_0^\top \mathbf{W} d_0$ is the weighted graph Laplacian. The potential $f^*$ is the unique solution lying in $(\ker \mathbf{L})^\perp$, and the cochain decomposes orthogonally as:
$$c = d_0 f^* + r^*$$
with $\langle d_0 f^*, r^* \rangle_\mathbf{W} = 0$ and Pythagorean energy conservation $\|c\|_{2, \mathbf{w}}^2 = \|d_0 f^*\|_{2, \mathbf{w}}^2 + \|r^*\|_{2, \mathbf{w}}^2$.

## 2. Context & Hypotheses

- **Graph Topology**: Finite 1-complex $G = (V, E)$.
- **Weight Matrix**: Positive diagonal matrix $\mathbf{W} = \operatorname{diag}(w_e)_{e \in E}$ with $w_e > 0$.
- **Coboundary Matrix**: $d_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{|E| \times |V|}$.
- **Weighted Inner Product on Cochains**: $\langle u, v \rangle_\mathbf{W} = u^\top \mathbf{W} v$.
- **Weighted Graph Laplacian**: $\mathbf{L} = d_0^\top \mathbf{W} d_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{|V| \times |V|}$.
- **Moore-Penrose Pseudoinverse**: $\mathbf{L}^+$ denotes the unique matrix satisfying the four Moore-Penrose equations: $\mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^+\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{L}$, $\mathbf{L}^+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^+ = \mathbf{L}^+$, $(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^+)^\top = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^+$, and $(\mathbf{L}^+\mathbf{L})^\top = \mathbf{L}^+\mathbf{L}$.

## 3. Derivation / Evidence

### 3.1 Normal Equations
1. The $L^2$ energy functional for potential $f$ is:
   $$J_2(f) = \frac{1}{2} \|c - d_0 f\|_{2, \mathbf{w}}^2 = \frac{1}{2} (c - d_0 f)^\top \mathbf{W} (c - d_0 f).$$
2. Expanding the quadratic form:
   $$J_2(f) = \frac{1}{2} c^\top \mathbf{W} c - f^\top (d_0^\top \mathbf{W} c) + \frac{1}{2} f^\top (d_0^\top \mathbf{W} d_0) f.$$
3. Computing the gradient with respect to $f$:
   $$\nabla J_2(f) = -d_0^\top \mathbf{W} c + \mathbf{L} f.$$
4. Setting $\nabla J_2(f) = 0$ yields the classical normal equations:
   $$\mathbf{L} f = d_0^\top \mathbf{W} c.$$

### 3.2 Solvability and Moore-Penrose Solution
1. The matrix $\mathbf{L}$ is real, symmetric, and positive semi-definite.
2. Its null space satisfies $\ker \mathbf{L} = \ker d_0$:
   $$f \in \ker \mathbf{L} \implies f^\top \mathbf{L} f = 0 \implies (d_0 f)^\top \mathbf{W} (d_0 f) = 0 \implies d_0 f = 0.$$
3. By the fundamental theorem of linear algebra:
   $$\operatorname{im} \mathbf{L} = (\ker \mathbf{L})^\perp = (\ker d_0)^\perp = \operatorname{im}(d_0^\top) = \operatorname{im}(d_0^\top \mathbf{W}).$$
4. Therefore, the right-hand side vector $b = d_0^\top \mathbf{W} c$ lies in $\operatorname{im} \mathbf{L}$, ensuring that the normal equations are consistent.
5. The Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse $\mathbf{L}^+$ projects onto the orthogonal complement of the kernel:
   $$\mathbf{L} \mathbf{L}^+ b = b \quad \text{for all } b \in \operatorname{im} \mathbf{L}.$$
6. Thus $f^* = \mathbf{L}^+ d_0^\top \mathbf{W} c$ satisfies $\mathbf{L} f^* = d_0^\top \mathbf{W} c$.
7. Furthermore, $\mathbf{L}^+$ selects the unique minimum Euclidean-norm solution in $(\ker \mathbf{L})^\perp$.

### 3.3 Weighted Hodge Decomposition and Pythagorean Conservation
1. Define the optimal residual:
   $$r^* = c - d_0 f^* = (\mathbf{I} - d_0 \mathbf{L}^+ d_0^\top \mathbf{W}) c.$$
2. Evaluating the divergence of $\mathbf{W} r^*$:
   $$d_0^\top \mathbf{W} r^* = d_0^\top \mathbf{W} c - (d_0^\top \mathbf{W} d_0) f^* = d_0^\top \mathbf{W} c - \mathbf{L} f^* = 0.$$
3. The exact component $d_0 f^*$ and residual $r^*$ are $\mathbf{W}$-orthogonal:
   $$\langle d_0 f^*, r^* \rangle_\mathbf{W} = (d_0 f^*)^\top \mathbf{W} r^* = (f^*)^\top (d_0^\top \mathbf{W} r^*) = (f^*)^\top 0 = 0.$$
4. Expanding the weighted $L^2$ norm:
   $$\|c\|_{2, \mathbf{w}}^2 = \|d_0 f^* + r^*\|_{2, \mathbf{w}}^2 = \|d_0 f^*\|_{2, \mathbf{w}}^2 + \|r^*\|_{2, \mathbf{w}}^2 + 2 \langle d_0 f^*, r^* \rangle_\mathbf{W} = \|d_0 f^*\|_{2, \mathbf{w}}^2 + \|r^*\|_{2, \mathbf{w}}^2.$$
5. This establishes the Pythagorean energy conservation of the Hodge-Helmholtz decomposition.

## 4. Limits and Non-Claims

- **$L^2$ Specificity**: Pythagorean energy conservation and linear Moore-Penrose solutions are specific to the Hilbertian $p = 2$ setting; they do not hold for non-Hilbertian $p \ne 2$.
- **Kernel Projection**: The Moore-Penrose potential $f^*$ satisfies $\sum_{v \in V_j} f^*(v) = 0$ on each component; other solutions differ by constants on each component.
- **Sparsity**: The $L^2$ residual $r^*$ is generally supported on all edges of a cycle and does not exhibit $L^1$ cycle sparsity.

## 5. Claim Hygiene Summary

1. **What it supports**:
   Supports the Moore-Penrose formula $f^* = \mathbf{L}^+ d_0^\top \mathbf{W} c$, weighted Hodge decomposition $c = d_0 f^* + r^*$, and Pythagorean energy conservation for $p = 2$ on finite graphs.
2. **What it does not support**:
   Does not support $L^1$ sparsity or linear pseudoinverse solutions for $p \ne 2$.
3. **Narrowest defensible claim**:
   For $p = 2$ and positive weights $\mathbf{W}$, $f^* = \mathbf{L}^+ d_0^\top \mathbf{W} c$ minimizes $\|c - d_0 f\|_{2, \mathbf{w}}$, yielding $\mathbf{W}$-orthogonal decomposition $c = d_0 f^* + r^*$.
