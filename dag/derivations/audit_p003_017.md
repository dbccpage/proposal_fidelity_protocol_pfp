## 1. Claim Statement

With 0 failures in $n$ independent $\text{Bernoulli}(p)$ trials, the one-sided 95% upper confidence bound on the failure probability $p$ is $1 - 0.05^{1/n}$.

## 2. Context & Hypotheses

- **Source Reference**:
  - `atlas/archive/pfp/003_pfp_host_admitted_failure_detectors/paper.md`, Section 7, lines 505–511.
  - `atlas/archive/pfp/003_pfp_host_admitted_failure_detectors/paper_claim_ledger.md`, row P3-17 (line 32).
- **Hypotheses**:
  - Observations comprise $n \in \mathbb{N}_{\ge 1}$ independent, identically distributed trials.
  - Each trial has a common binary outcome with failure probability $p \in [0, 1]$.
  - The observed count of failures is exactly $K = 0$.
- **Target Confidence Level**:
  - One-sided coverage probability $1 - \alpha = 0.95$, corresponding to significance level $\alpha = 0.05 = 1/20$.
- **Obligations**:
  - Independence and identical distribution of trials are strictly assumed.
  - Correlated proposal distributions, shared prompt templates, or repeated fixtures violate independence and invalidate direct application of the bound.

## 3. Derivation / Evidence

### 3.1 Exact Bernoulli Likelihood for Zero Failures
For $n$ independent and identically distributed Bernoulli trials with failure parameter $p$, the probability of observing exactly $k$ failures follows the binomial distribution:
$$P(K = k; n, p) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1 - p)^{n - k}$$
When zero failures are observed ($k = 0$):
$$P(K = 0; n, p) = (1 - p)^n$$

### 3.2 Monotonicity and Confidence Interval Inversion
For any fixed integer $n \ge 1$, the function $f(p) = (1 - p)^n$ is strictly decreasing on the interval $[0, 1]$:
$$\frac{d}{dp}(1 - p)^n = -n(1 - p)^{n - 1} < 0 \quad \text{for } p \in [0, 1)$$
The exact Clopper-Pearson one-sided $1 - \alpha$ confidence region consists of all parameter values $p$ for which the probability of observing an outcome as extreme as $K = 0$ is at least $\alpha$:
$$\{p \in [0, 1] : P(K \le 0; n, p) \ge \alpha\}$$
Since $K$ is non-negative, $P(K \le 0; n, p) = P(K = 0; n, p) = (1 - p)^n$. The condition becomes:
$$(1 - p)^n \ge \alpha$$
Taking the $n$-th root on both sides (since $1 - p \ge 0$ and $\alpha > 0$):
$$1 - p \ge \alpha^{1/n} \iff p \le 1 - \alpha^{1/n}$$
Setting $\alpha = 0.05$:
$$p_{\text{upper}} = 1 - 0.05^{1/n}$$

### 3.3 Rule of Three Approximation
Using the series expansion $\ln(1 - p) = -p - \mathcal{O}(p^2)$ for small $p$:
$$(1 - p)^n = \exp(n \ln(1 - p)) \approx \exp(-np)$$
Equating $\exp(-np) = 0.05$ yields:
$$-np \approx \ln(0.05) = -\ln(20) \approx -2.99573$$
$$p \approx \frac{2.99573}{n} \approx \frac{3}{n}$$
This provides the standard rule-of-three heuristic for the upper bound.

### 3.4 Exact Rational Checkpoints
Paper 003 Section 7 cites two concrete numerical points:
1. **Eight observations ($n = 8$)**:
   - The exact upper bound is $p \le 1 - (1/20)^{1/8}$.
   - Evaluating with exact rational arithmetic:
     - At $p = 31/100$: $(1 - 31/100)^8 = (69/100)^8 = 513798374428641 / 10000000000000000 \approx 0.05138 > 1/20$.
     - At $p = 32/100$: $(1 - 32/100)^8 = (17/25)^8 = 6975757441 / 152587890625 \approx 0.04572 < 1/20$.
   - Thus, the exact bound falls in the open rational interval $(31/100, 32/100)$, leaving an upper bound near 31%.
2. **Sub-1% requirement ($p_{\text{upper}} < 1/100$)**:
   - Testing $p = 1/100$:
     - For $n = 298$: $(1 - 1/100)^{298} = (99/100)^{298} \approx 0.0500366 > 1/20$.
     - For $n = 299$: $(1 - 1/100)^{299} = (99/100)^{299} \approx 0.0495363 < 1/20$.
   - Hence, $n = 299$ is the minimal integer sample size required to bring the 95% one-sided bound on $p$ strictly below $1/100$.

## 4. Limits and Non-Claims

1. **Dependence Invalidation**: The bound holds strictly under independent and identically distributed Bernoulli trials. As Paper 003 emphasizes, shared model prompts, correlated responses, and reused test fixtures violate independence and prevent interpreting these numbers as empirical safety metrics.
2. **Planning Model Only**: This formula represents a sample-size planning benchmark, not an empirical assertion that any evaluated system has achieved a failure rate below 1%.
3. **One-Sided Formulation**: The bound is strictly an upper confidence limit; observing zero failures in a finite sample does not establish that the true failure rate is zero.

## 5. Claim Hygiene Summary

1. **What it supports**: Derives the exact mathematical relation $p \le 1 - 0.05^{1/n}$ for zero failures under independent Bernoulli sampling, establishing that $n = 8$ yields a bound near 31% and $n = 299$ is the minimal sample size bringing the bound below 1%.
2. **What it does not support**: Does not support claims that actual LLM failure detector trials satisfy independence, nor that Paper 003 establishes empirical failure rates below 1%.
3. **Narrowest defensible claim**: Under the declared assumption of $n$ independent Bernoulli trials with zero observed failures, the Clopper-Pearson 95% upper confidence bound on $p$ is $1 - 0.05^{1/n}$.
4. **Next test needed**: Non-parametric bootstrap and permutation tests on empirical proposal streams to evaluate the extent of intra-cluster correlation.
