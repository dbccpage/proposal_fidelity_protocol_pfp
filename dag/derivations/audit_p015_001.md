## 2. Hypotheses

1. **Finite carrier and declared domains**: \(V\) is a finite variable set, each \(v \in V\) has a finite declared domain \(D_v\), and \((S_i)_{i \in I}\) is a finite family of scopes \(S_i \subseteq V\) with covered union \(U = \bigcup_{i \in I} S_i\).
2. **Local permission relations**: For each \(i \in I\), \(R_i \subseteq \prod_{v \in S_i} D_v\) is an explicit allowed relation, and a locally permitted family selects \(s_i \in R_i\) for every \(i \in I\).
3. **Locality of global satisfaction**: When \(\bigcup_{i \in I} S_i = V\), a global assignment \(g \in \prod_{v \in V} D_v\) is a global solution iff \(g|_{S_i} \in R_i\) for all \(i \in I\).

## 3. Step-by-Step Derivation

### Step 1 — Necessity of pairwise overlap agreement

Suppose a family \((s_i : S_i \to \bigsqcup_{v \in S_i} D_v)_{i \in I}\) glues to an assignment \(g\) on \(U = \bigcup_{i \in I} S_i\), meaning \(g|_{S_i} = s_i\) for every \(i \in I\). For any indices \(i, j \in I\) and any \(v \in S_i \cap S_j\),

\[
s_i(v) = (g|_{S_i})(v) = g(v) = (g|_{S_j})(v) = s_j(v).
\]

Hence \(s_i|_{S_i \cap S_j} = s_j|_{S_i \cap S_j}\) for every pair \((i, j)\).

### Step 2 — Sufficiency and uniqueness on \(\bigcup_i S_i\)

Assume \(s_i|_{S_i \cap S_j} = s_j|_{S_i \cap S_j}\) for all \(i, j \in I\). Fix a selector \(\kappa : U \to I\) choosing for each \(v \in U\) an index \(\kappa(v) \in I\) with \(v \in S_{\kappa(v)}\), and define \(g(v) = s_{\kappa(v)}(v) \in D_v\).

- **Extension property**: For any \(i \in I\) and \(v \in S_i\), we have \(v \in S_{\kappa(v)} \cap S_i\), so pairwise compatibility yields \(g(v) = s_{\kappa(v)}(v) = s_i(v)\). Thus \(g|_{S_i} = s_i\) for every \(i \in I\).
- **Uniqueness on \(U\)**: If \(g', g''\) both satisfy \(g'|_{S_i} = s_i = g''|_{S_i}\) for all \(i \in I\), then for every \(v \in U\), \(v \in S_{\kappa(v)}\) implies \(g'(v) = s_{\kappa(v)}(v) = g''(v)\), so \(g' = g''\) pointwise on \(U\).

### Step 3 — Bijection with global solutions when \(\bigcup_i S_i = V\)

Assume \(U = V\), let \(\mathcal{G} = \{g \in \prod_{v \in V} D_v : \forall i \in I,\; g|_{S_i} \in R_i\}\) be the set of global solutions, and let

\[
\mathcal{F} = \Big\{(s_i)_{i \in I} \in \prod_{i \in I} R_i : \forall i, j \in I,\; s_i|_{S_i \cap S_j} = s_j|_{S_i \cap S_j}\Big\}
\]

be the set of pairwise-compatible locally permitted families. Define \(\rho : \mathcal{G} \to \mathcal{F}\) by \(\rho(g) = (g|_{S_i})_{i \in I}\) and \(\gamma : \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{G}\) by the unique glued assignment \(\gamma((s_i)_{i \in I}) = g\) from Step 2:

1. **Well-definedness of \(\rho\)**: If \(g \in \mathcal{G}\), then \(g|_{S_i} \in R_i\) by definition of \(\mathcal{G}\), and pairwise agreement holds by Step 1, so \(\rho(g) \in \mathcal{F}\).
2. **Well-definedness of \(\gamma\)**: If \((s_i)_{i \in I} \in \mathcal{F}\), Step 2 gives \(\gamma((s_i))|_{S_i} = s_i \in R_i\) for every \(i \in I\), so \(\gamma((s_i)) \in \mathcal{G}\).
3. **Two-sided inverse**: For \(g \in \mathcal{G}\), \(\gamma(\rho(g))\) restricts to \(g|_{S_i}\) on each \(S_i\); since \(g\) also restricts to \(g|_{S_i}\) and \(U = V\), uniqueness in Step 2 forces \(\gamma(\rho(g)) = g\). Conversely, for \((s_i)_{i \in I} \in \mathcal{F}\), \(\rho(\gamma((s_i)))_i = \gamma((s_i))|_{S_i} = s_i\) for every \(i \in I\), so \(\rho(\gamma((s_i))) = (s_i)_{i \in I}\).

### Step 4 — Uncovered-variable boundary cases (Paper 015 lines 54–56)

When \(U \subsetneq V\) and \(v_0 \in V \setminus U\):
- If \(|D_{v_0}| \ge 2\), a glued assignment on \(U\) admits \(|D_{v_0}| \ge 2\) distinct extensions to \(V\), defeating uniqueness on \(V\) unless a unary scope \(\{v_0\}\) is supplied.
- If \(D_{v_0} = \emptyset\), \(\prod_{v \in V} D_v = \emptyset\) even when every covered \(R_i\) is nonempty and compatible, defeating global existence unless full-domain unary relations cover \(V\).
