# Lesson 1: What Is a Triangle?

## Learning Objectives

1. Define triangle as three non-collinear points connected by straight lines
2. Understand SSS, SAS, ASA congruence criteria
3. Calculate angles using the cosine rule
4. Apply triangles to physical problems

## Four-Part Structure

| Part | Title | Sections | Focus |
| ------ | ------- | ---------- | ------- |
| I | Foundations | 1-24 | Definition, properties, basic calculations |
| II | The Math of the Turn | 25-48 | Rotation, trigonometry, coordinate geometry |
| III | Applications | 49-72 | Physics, engineering, navigation |
| IV | Extensions | 73-84 | Higher dimensions, non-Euclidean geometry |

## Key Formulas

| Name | Formula | Use Case |
| ------ | --------- | ---------- |
| Law of Cosines | c² = a² + b² - 2ab·cos(C) | Find angle from three sides |
| Law of Sines | a/sin(A) = b/sin(B) = c/sin(C) | Find sides from angles |
| Heron's Formula | A = √(s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)) | Area from three sides |

## Physical Anchors

- **Bridge truss** - triangular frames for rigidity
- **GPS triangulation** - position from three distances
- **Surveyor's transit** - measuring angles to triangulate positions

## Lexicon

- **Triangle** - polygon with three sides
- **Congruence** - identical shape and size
- **SSS** - Side-Side-Side uniqueness theorem

## One-Minute Summary

Triangles are the simplest polygon and the only rigid one. Three connected
points form a unique shape with predictable properties. The Law of Cosines
lets us calculate any angle or side.
