# 1.06 Why Triangles Are Rigid

**Section:** 6 of 84
**Duration:** ~15 minutes
**Part:** I — Foundations

---

## Big Idea

Nature is lazy, but likes to be certain.

When you lock in three side lengths of a triangle, you lock in everything else —
angles, area, shape. No wiggle room.

This is the **SSS uniqueness theorem**: if two triangles have the same three
side lengths, they must be identical (congruent).

---

## Why This Matters

A square with fixed side lengths can still wobble into a rhombus. But a triangle
can't change at all once you fix its sides.

This is why:

- Bridges use triangular trusses
- Bicycle frames are triangulated
- Crane jibs are made of triangles

$$\text{Rigidity} = \text{No degrees of freedom}$$

---

## Summary

Triangles are the only rigid polygon. Once you fix the three side lengths,
the shape is completely determined. This makes triangles the fundamental
building block for stable structures.
