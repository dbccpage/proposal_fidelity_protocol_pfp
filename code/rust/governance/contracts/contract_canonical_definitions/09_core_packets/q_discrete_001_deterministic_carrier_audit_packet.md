Thesis
------

Deterministic and discrete carrier proposals (including 't Hooft's cellular automaton interpretation) are representation-transport challenges, not ontological debates. The correct intake is: demand the packet. A deterministic carrier must supply finite shadow dynamics, a representation transport map into QM formalism, and a Bell/contextuality/locality accounting packet before being admitted beyond Q.

Driving obstruction
-------------------

The Core QM spine treats QM as a finite obstruction-descent calculus. Deterministic/discrete proposals claim that the same finite predictions can be recovered from a deterministic substrate. The central question for Core is not "is nature deterministic?" but:

```text
Does the deterministic carrier supply a finite shadow,
a checkable representation transport into QM predictions,
and a complete accounting of Bell/contextuality/locality residuals?
```

Background: 't Hooft's program
------------------------------

Gerard 't Hooft's "Cellular Automaton Interpretation of Quantum Mechanics" (Springer, 2016; arXiv:1405.1548) presents quantum mechanics as a tool for analyzing underlying deterministic dynamics. Key claims:

1. A deterministic system with a discrete, finite or countable state space can be analyzed using quantum mechanical techniques (Hilbert space embedding, unitary evolution).
2. The quantum states are a representation tool, not necessarily the ontology.
3. Beables (determinate states at all times) can be identified within the Hilbert space formalism.
4. The Born rule emerges from time-averaging or coarse-graining of the deterministic dynamics.
5. Bell's theorem is addressed by superdeterminism / conspiracy of initial conditions.

Support boundary
----------------

* **Supports:** The deterministic carrier is a legitimate representation-transport candidate. Core should absorb it as a packet, not argue ontology.
* **Does not support:** Claiming the deterministic carrier is refuted or confirmed by the Core spine. The spine demands transport and accounting, not metaphysical selection.
* **Narrowest defensible claim:** Deterministic proposals are Q-held with named debt (Bell accounting, Born-rule derivation, representation transport) until the accounting packet is supplied and passes the finite-prediction comparison.
* **Next test needed:** Construct the minimal 't Hooft cellular automaton on a finite lattice, compute its Hilbert-space embedding, and compare finite-packet predictions against standard QM on CHSH and Mermin-Peres tests.

Q-layer status
--------------

The 't Hooft cellular automaton *on a finite lattice* does not need Q admission (finite carrier). The *continuum limit* claim (if made) would require Q admission with the four continuum-quarantine criteria. The superdeterminism accounting is not a Q question; it is a Bell/locality accounting question that is either supplied or refused.
