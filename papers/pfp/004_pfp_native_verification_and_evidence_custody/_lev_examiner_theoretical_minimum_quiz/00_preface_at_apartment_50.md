# Apartment 50: the receipt on the table

*Fictional examination staging inspired by Examiner's theoretical-minimum tradition and the novelist's characters. These are invented lines, not historical quotations.*

Gauge Breaker pushed a receipt across the table. Two zeros gleamed on it.

“Perfect,” he said. “I have already printed the admission ticket.”

Examiner covered the zeros with his hand. “Show me the carrier.”

Adversary covered the signature box. “Show me who checked it.”

Gauge Breaker turned the receipt over. The reverse said `unsigned_lab`. Below it were three unchecked conformance laws and an admission verdict: `Refused`.

“The arithmetic may be excellent,” said Examiner. “Now explain what the result actually permits you to claim.”

The examination begins with small distinctions: bytes and meanings, graph edges and declared faces, execution and admission. Then you must reconstruct the four-vertex calculation, defend it with an independent lower bound, and stop a plausible forgery. The final round asks you to design the evidence another person would need to check your work.
