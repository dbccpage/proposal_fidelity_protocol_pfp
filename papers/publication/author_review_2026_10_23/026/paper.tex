\subsection{Abstract}\label{abstract}

Equal current answers need not permit equal future choices. For a finite
deterministic transition table, we characterize an exact operation and
cost quotient and construct the coarsest stable refinement of an initial
report. We give counterexamples to output-only compression and to
choosing the fewest operations as the cheapest solution. The finite
result imports standard transition-system refinement; its WDP role is
preserving the original future question during representation change.

\subsection{1. The declared finite
model}\label{the-declared-finite-model}

Let \(X\) be a finite state set, \(A\) a finite action alphabet, and
\(q_0\) an initial report. Each action \(a\) has a partial deterministic
transition \(t_a\) and a positive rational cost \(c_a\) on its domain.
Terminal task labels are included in the protected report. A quotient
\(q:X\to Z\) is exact if all action domains, costs, successor reports,
and terminal labels descend and reflect the declared concrete
operations.

\subsection{2. Necessary and sufficient structural
conditions}\label{necessary-and-sufficient-structural-conditions}

\textbf{Theorem.} Such an exact quotient exists on a supplied report
\(q\) precisely when states in one fibre have the same terminal labels
and, for each action, the same enablement, the same enabled cost, and
the same successor fibre.

\textbf{Proof.} If quotient operations descend and reflect, evaluating
one quotient state cannot depend on which representative was chosen.
Each listed condition follows. Conversely, define enabled quotient
actions, their costs, and their successors using any
\nolinkurl{representative.} The conditions make all these definitions
independent of the \nolinkurl{representative.} They give the commuting
step maps and reflection of action enablement. Induction on a word then
gives identical legal action words, protected reports, and accumulated
costs for states in one fibre. Consequently the best finite resolution
cost is also preserved, including the absence of any legal resolving
word.

This theorem concerns a deterministic table. For adversarial outcomes,
the required successor data are the complete declared outcome relations
in the appropriate information-state arena; Appendix S states that
stronger model. Deterministic examples cannot certify a stochastic or
hidden-state adapter.

\subsection{3. Coarsest stable
refinement}\label{coarsest-stable-refinement}

Start from \(q_0\)'s partition, including terminal labels. Repeatedly
split each block by the action signature: disabled, or the pair
consisting of exact cost and successor block. Each proper split
increases the number of blocks, so at most \(|X|-1\) proper splits
occur. A stable partition satisfies the theorem's conditions. Any stable
refinement of \(q_0\) refines every iteration: prove this by induction
using its equal costs and successor classes. Therefore the terminal
partition is the coarsest stable refinement. This is a finite
construction, not a physically supplied observation instrument.

\subsection{4. Current-output and cost
counterexamples}\label{current-output-and-cost-counterexamples}

Two states \(a,\allowbreak b\) both report zero, and both allow
\passthrough{\lstinline!finish!}, reaching the same terminal state. Its
cost is one at \(a\) and three at \(b\). The current output is preserved
by identifying them, but operating cost is not. Disabling
\passthrough{\lstinline!finish!} at \(b\) also destroys future
capability. Conversely, a task asking only for the current zero has the
same answer in both states despite these structural failures: accidental
task-value preservation is weaker than operation-preserving exactness.

In a second table, \passthrough{\lstinline!direct!} reaches the terminal
in one operation costing five. \passthrough{\lstinline!next!} and
\passthrough{\lstinline!finish!} take two operations costing one each.
The cheaper route costs two. Add a positive-cost self-loop: it cannot
improve any finite resolving word because deleting its repeated-state
segment gives a legal word of strictly smaller cost. Thus an optimum,
when reachable, occurs among simple paths of at most \(|X|-1\)
transitions in this deterministic model.

\subsection{5. Replay and scope}\label{replay-and-scope}

The cost example declares states \passthrough{\lstinline!r!},
\passthrough{\lstinline!s!} and terminal \passthrough{\lstinline!t!}:
\passthrough{\lstinline!r!} can go directly to
\passthrough{\lstinline!t!} for 5, move to \passthrough{\lstinline!s!}
for 1 and then finish for 1, or loop at \passthrough{\lstinline!r!} for
1. The checker replays the submitted route and its claimed rational
price. A separate procedure enumerates simple paths in the original
table. Positive cycles can be erased from a terminating deterministic
route, so that finite enumeration establishes the minimum 2. A falsely
claimed price, an expensive route presented as optimal and an unfinished
route are rejected.

\subsection{Research extension: bounded context without a preservation
claim}\label{research-extension-bounded-context-without-a-preservation-claim}

The AIDE2-inspired reference replaces full attempt history with the
root, champion, two recent attempt summaries and references to retained
evidence. The host refuses a projection that drops required references.
Failure guidance is scoped and capped. These are bounded operational
choices, not a construction of the coarsest stable quotient defined
above. The external paper reports prompt size reductions for its own
benchmarks (p.~9); those values are not local results.

Consider two resumption records with the same displayed answer and
payload digest. One retains the artifact reference, task and state
binding; the other keeps only the digest. A later resume operation can
be enabled on the first and refused on the second. A shorter summary
that identifies them therefore violates future-operation preservation
even if their current displayed answers agree. Keeping a textual
reference is necessary for the campaign's custody contract, but is not
sufficient to prove that a stochastic model will use the referenced
content correctly. Equal token counts likewise imply no equality of
future costs, action availability or successor states.

The campaign records context bytes, actual reported input and output
tokens, completion, failures and cost. The no-compression ablation keeps
full prior code within the same request ceiling; crossing that ceiling
is a retained refusal. This compares bounded policies rather than
granting one arm an unlimited context. A reduction in context size may
accompany a loss of useful distinctions, and a correct answer on the
measured panel does not discharge every future operation.

The existing finite deterministic theorem and its replay are reused
unchanged. No new Lean result is claimed. Since the live campaign has
not run, this revision reports no local compression factor, cost saving
or preservation measurement.
